## Strongest Counter-Argument

The paper's title asserts a training-data format claim, and the manuscript's own measurements refute it in both directions at once. As a training-format claim, the entire benefit of composition over per-block indexing is one question out of 60 (11.7% vs 10.0%, 7 versus 6 correct), and that margin is presented as an ordering that "holds on every stratum" when Table 2 shows ties on two of the three strata. The paper's own citations (Allen-Zhu, Li et al.) say the 25,755-token regime cannot extract facts seen in few forms, so the head-to-head was designed, by construction, to be unable to detect a format effect; a foregone negative is not evidence about training-data format. As a graph claim, the only retrieval figure the graph earns unaided (53.3%) is matched and edged by a from-scratch BM25 over raw estate bytes with no entities, no resolution, and no graph (60.0%). The sole arm that clears BM25 (81.7%) does so by consuming the asked person's correctly resolved identity from the benchmark manifest, on an estate where the paper elsewhere measures exact-match identity resolution at 24.27% pairwise recall. That is the headline number conditional on assuming solved the problem the paper itself declares the open measurement. Subtract the oracle, subtract the one-question format margin, and the honest summary of Table 2 is: a lexical retriever built in a few hundred lines does everything the five-kernel, 388,797-byte stack is measured to do, and a span-provenance ledger over raw text would deliver the erasure byte-diff without an entity graph at all. The measured value the artifact alone provides is, so far, undemonstrated.

## Issue List

1. **CRITICAL | Internal consistency / logic chain** [Section "The reading, recorded as it ran", paragraph 2; Table 2 (the ten-arm table); Section "The Six-Arm Head-to-Head" claim 3; Conclusion]: The text claims "the design's ordering holds on every stratum: composed (11.7%) over per-block (10.0%) over flat (6.7%)" and the Conclusion repeats "composition wins over per-block and flat formats on every stratum by small margins." Table 2 contradicts this: on Q1 per-block and composed tie at 8.3%, on Q2 they tie at 12.5%; composed is strictly above per-block only on Q3 (16.7% vs 8.3%), where per-block ties flat (8.3%). The per-stratum "ordering" is two ties and one cell; "wins on every stratum" is false against the paper's own table. This is the paper's only positive evidence for the training-format thesis, so the defect is load-bearing.

2. **CRITICAL | Unsupported premise feeding the headline** [Abstract; Section "Non-training controls"; Section "Uncertainty at n=60"]: The 81.7% arm, called "the strongest arm measured" in the abstract and conclusion, receives the asked person's canonical identity key from the manifest (artifact note: "the manifest is read by this runner only to map question personIds to cache records"). The paper concedes the scoping in prose but the number still leads the abstract and the conclusion as the graph's strongest payoff. The premise that a deployer starts from a correctly resolved identity is exactly the paper's own declared-open problem (Stage K5 pending; exact-match recall 24.27% on this estate). A premise a core headline depends on and that is measured as unsolved is a CRITICAL row, not a prose caveat.

3. **MAJOR | Alternative paths / "so what"** [Section "The estate"; results artifact `track-k7-cag-vs-rag-vs-bm25.json`]: All 60 questions are single-person, single-attribute keyed lookups across exactly four attribute families (worksIn 24, ownsProject 12, titled 12, joinedOn 12 in the artifact rows). Every answer is one roster/table field. The natural competing baseline, a deterministic person-to-field lookup built the way BM25's passage pool was built, answers these questions without any language model and is never run or discussed. Measuring LLM arms on a lookup regime and citing 81.7% as a win leaves open that the entire comparison measures the wrong baseline.

4. **MAJOR | Logic chain / premature closure** [Section "Introduction", item 3; Section "The Erasure Property"; Section "What This Paper Does Not Claim" item 1 of the intro's framing "each is a measured receipt"]: The paper enumerates three load-bearing properties and states each is "a measured receipt." The third, erasability, is half-measured: the corpus half has the byte-diff receipts, but the adapter half (the half that makes the GDPR Article 17 chain close, the paper's own framing) is explicitly "pending GPU backfill." Until retrain-versus-scratch, SISA comparison, and pre/post canary exposure are measured, "erasable ... completes the erasure path" is a designed structure, not a measured property. The framing "measured receipts" overstates it.

5. **MAJOR | Circularity in the cache-arm comparison** [Section "Non-training controls", CAG paragraph; artifact `cacheUnitSelectionRule`]: The fixed-cache arm's 8 records were chosen by "greedy set-cover over the 52 unique question persons," that is, the cache contents are optimized against the evaluation question population itself. No deployment knows the future question distribution, so 51.7% is an eval-informed upper bound for that arm, and the paper's reading of CAG's per-stratum behavior ("a fixed cache cannot fetch the one document") is based on a selection procedure unavailable in deployment. Disclosed in one clause, not framed as a validity threat.

6. **MAJOR | Design insufficient for its own claim** [Section "Honesty boundaries of this measurement"; Section "What This Paper Does Not Claim" item 3]: The paper admits the regime is capacity-starved and repetition-starved, and that budgets beyond the sweep are open work; yet the abstract retains "composition's format benefit is directionally positive and small." A one-question margin inside a regime the paper argues cannot express the effect does not license "directionally positive" as a property of composition; it licenses "undetectable here." The abstract's directional claim should be absent until a budget that can express the effect is run (measurement needed: trained-arm comparison at one substantially larger budget, e.g., 10x tokens).

7. **MAJOR | Tautological measurement framed as a cost quantification** [Section "The reading, recorded as it ran", bullet 4; Section "The Sample Schema"]: The vaulted ablation is scored on closed-book identity questions while the same section states the vault "masks 36.31% of subwords" and that loss on vault spans is masked. The 1.7% result is guaranteed by the view's construction; calling it "the privacy cost quantified" presents a definitional outcome as an empirical cost. A real privacy-cost measurement would need utility questions the vaulted view can in principle answer (structure, counts, relations) or a task battery indifferent to identity, and neither is run.

8. **MAJOR | Overgeneralization from one dev-set number** [Section "Cross-department linking", final sentence]: "the estate's own PPRL benchmark measures a 4.88% false-link rate at its dev threshold on a 500-common-names collision set, and that is the regime real estates live in." A single threshold on a single synthetic collision set is generalized to all real estates in one clause. This clause then does quiet work: it is what makes the 14.32x false-merge amplification read as a deployable tradeoff figure. The premise that 4.88% is representative is unsupported and needs its own row.

9. **MINOR | Spurious precision dressing small samples** [Abstract; Table 1 caption area; Section "Detection and certificates"; Section "Failure Modes"]: 95.8333% (23 of 24), 81.7108% certified lower bound on 24 samples, 14.32 and 12.79 amplification factors from induced-merge ladders. The paper itself calls the bound "small-sample arithmetic, not evidence," yet the abstract prints the certificate rate and its four-decimal certified floor as operating properties. Four decimals on n=24 is presentation of certainty the data does not hold.

10. **MINOR | Judge columns imply adjudication they cannot provide** [Table 2 (the ten-arm table); Section "The design"]: Row agreement between the two judges is 6.7% to 41.7% across arms and the strict judge scores every trained arm at or below base (0.03 to 0.23 against base 0.03). The Llama column scores base (0.98) above the strict scores of all trained arms, so the two columns rank arms incompatibly. Carrying them as columns in the main table without a stated decision rule for conflicts invites readers to average noise.

## Ignored Alternative Explanations and Paths

- **Roster lookup without a model.** The dominant alternative path for this question regime; see Issue 3. If a keyed lookup scores near 100% modulo planted noise, every number in the ten-arm table is a measure of how far each arm is from a dictionary, not of training-data format.
- **Full-estate prefill.** The paper cites Li et al. (2024) bounding full-context versus retrieval but never runs the obvious arm for an estate this flat corpus fits near: prefill all 115 documents' text up to the context limit, no graph, no entity resolution. If that arm lands near the hybrid's 81.7%, both the graph and composition are unnecessary for the measured value.
- **Retrieval-restricted routing without the graph.** The proper control for the hybrid arm is BM25 with each question's person string as a filter over the same 216-passage pool. If person-filtered BM25 reaches 81.7%, the entity graph and the composed records contribute nothing to the strongest arm; the arm's gain is then attributable to question-time selection, not to grounded training data.
- **Train on retrieved contexts.** The design's real competitor, fine-tuning on the retriever's top-6 contexts (retrieval-augmented SFT), is absent. Composition is only compared against other offline formats, never against the format most likely to combine both mechanisms' advantages.
- **Noise, not displacement, explains the pairs arm.** "Composed + pairs" scoring exactly base is attributed to budget displacement with token counts; an unexamined rival is that contrastive pairs at any budget actively hurt closed-book EM at this scale, which would matter to any reader considering the pair idea independently.

## Missing Stakeholder Perspectives

- **The falsely merged data subject.** The 14.32x amplification is presented as corpus corruption from the trainer's side. From the person's side, composition renders their facts under another anchor and vice versa; the per-individual distribution of that harm (how many people, how many wrong facts each) is never reported, though the ladder data in `composed-corpus-failure-modes.json` could produce it.
- **The deployer without a manifest.** Every strong arm in the paper's ecosystem implicitly assumes truth-side identity exists somewhere; the paper's own K5 line is the open measurement that a real deployer would need first, and the paper never states the order of operations (resolve first, then everything measured here works).
- **The consumer of the vaulted default.** Since "the training loss sees identity only where a view renders it into tokens," the default-view corpus's "records that know what they describe" benefit the pipeline's own machinery, not the model. The beneficiary of the flagship property is the erasure function itself; who, outside the paper's seam, reads the entity metadata is never addressed.

## Observations (Non-Defects)

These are stated so the synthesizer does not re-litigate them:
- **Perception Table 1's zero baselines** are construction validation, and the paper says so in three places, including inside the table caption and the "uniform high columns validate the construction, not generalization" scoping sentence. Not a hidden defect.
- **The second estate** is explicitly framed as floor-machinery validation only, with Layer R's 22.79% precision reported raw and Enron's licensing status disclosed. The role boundary is honest as written.
- **Interval reporting at n=60** is full and self-undermining where warranted; the paper does not hide that retrieval arms are mutually indistinguishable.
- **The pairs-arm failure explanation** (budget displacement, 50 rows / 9,947 learnable tokens) is artifact-backed and internally coherent; read it as an explanation, not an excuse to challenge.
- **The "4.6x" separation** (53.3% vs 11.7%) is the one trained-versus-untrained gap with non-overlapping intervals; the paper's use of it is statistically legitimate even though the framing around it (Issues 1, 2) is not.

## Summary for the synthesizer

The two rows that decide the paper are Issue 1 (the "every stratum" claim is contradicted by the ten-arm table's ties) and Issue 2 (the headline arm is oracle-conditioned on a problem the paper itself declares open). Everything else is weight on the same beam: what survives every control run here is a lexical retriever, not grounded training data, and the artifact's distinctive value claims (adapter erasure, resolution, deployable routing) are all either pending measurements or premises the paper has not measured at all.
