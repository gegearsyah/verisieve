\begin{abstract}
Enterprises sit on scattered, heterogeneous documents (SQL dumps, spreadsheets, email, meeting transcripts and audio, decks, notes), and the standard path from those documents to fine-tuning data is positional chunking: slice each file into windows, concatenate, train. The result is training data that does not know what it describes: text at a position, with no record of which real-world entities it mentions, which other documents describe the same entities, or where its bytes came from. This paper presents what training data should look like instead: a \textbf{graph-grounded training corpus} in which every sample is standalone knowledge, a record that knows its own entities, their classes, and its provenance byte-ranges into the source documents, so that cross-department, cross-modal mentions of the same entity carry one identity and facts scattered across the estate become learnable as one unit. The stack that builds it is a zero-host-import, zero-egress perception pipeline of five WebAssembly kernels totaling $388{,}797$\,B, and the operating properties an enterprise needs ride along quietly: values are vaulted behind salted placeholders by default, every sample carries a gatekeeper certificate bounding residual detectable PII at a stated confidence, and deletion regenerates the corpus exactly. On a deterministic 12-file, 4-department synthetic estate, the pipeline indexes 24 ground-truth entities into 76 corpus entities across 8 modalities; all 54 ground-truth cross-department links are recovered (value-level exact matching on planted values: precision, recall, and F1 at $100\%$); the per-sample certificate pass rate is $95.8333\%$ (certified Clopper--Pearson lower bound $81.7108\%$ at stated $95\%$); and deletion requests over 1\%, 5\%, and 20\% of entities regenerate a corpus whose byte-diff is exactly the erased spans, with a sealed LoRA retrain recipe completing the erasure path for the adapter trained on it. The corpus seam additionally emits a relational composition of itself, one training sample per resolved entity, and the downstream comparison this artifact exists for is measured, not promised: on a noisy 115-document estate with 60 manifest questions at an equal token budget, retrieval over the same entity graph with no fine-tuning answers $53.3\%$ of questions exactly ($83.3\%$ on the cross-document stratum), while the best trained format, the composed corpus, reaches $11.7\%$ against $10.0\%$ per-block, $6.7\%$ flat chunks, and $1.7\%$ for the untrained base; the composed corpus also amplifies false-merge damage by a factor of $14.32$ over per-block when entity resolution is wrong, and co-mention reuse makes near-duplicates of the whole composed corpus. Three non-training controls complete the comparison: a lexical BM25 control ties the graph retriever ($60.0\%$ against $53.3\%$, overlapping $95\%$ intervals at $n{=}60$), so the graph's contribution on this estate is not retrieval quality; the composed records cached as one fixed unit tie the retrieval arms ($51.7\%$); and routing each question to the asked person's records, the graph-as-router reading, answers $81.7\%$, the strongest arm measured, with the scoping that routing consumes a correctly resolved identity rather than doing retrieval. External validity is receipted on a second estate built from openly licensed AMI and ICSI meeting transcripts with an overlay cast, where the trivial exact-match floor fails as designed (pairwise recall $61.46\%$, $2{,}303$ false merges on designed collision pairs). We publish the honest boundaries of these receipts: the fixtures are synthetic or overlay-by-construction; the certificate is not differential privacy; and the measured comparison runs a 1.5B-parameter adapter in a capacity-starved regime, where the graph pays off as a retrieval index and, in the strongest measured arm, as a router, composition's format benefit is directionally positive and small, and its failure modes are quantified rather than hidden.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The dominant pipeline from enterprise documents to language-model training data is \emph{place and stitch}: chunk each document by position, optionally overlap the windows, concatenate the chunks, and train. The chunk inherits its meaning from where it sits; stripped of that position, it is a fragment. Two records about the same person (a vendor row in a SQL dump and a signature block in an email thread) land in unrelated samples with no shared identity. The model is then asked to learn facts from text whose entities are anonymous to the pipeline that produced it. The question this paper takes seriously is the one upstream of every fine-tuning run: what should the training data itself look like?

This paper describes the construction of the answer: a corpus whose samples are \emph{standalone knowledge}. A sample stands on its own because it is a record that knows what it describes. Alongside its text (values masked behind salted placeholders by default), it carries the stable identifiers of every entity it mentions; the classes of those entities; its byte-range provenance into the source document; and a per-sample certificate from a conformal gate attesting, at a stated confidence, that the sample carries no residual detectable PII. Records about the same real-world person, across departments and across modalities, share an identifier, so the corpus has a graph, not just a sequence.

Three properties are load-bearing for this artifact, and each is a measured receipt rather than an adjective:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Extracted exactly on the estate, on-device.} The perception stack is five WebAssembly kernels totaling $388{,}797$\,B with zero host imports, running with $0$\,B of network egress; the whole corpus build is instrumented with trap-armed network surfaces that would throw on any external call. ``Exactly'' is construction validation on the synthetic estate (Section~\ref{sec:measurements}), not an open-world accuracy claim.
  \item \textbf{Grounded in its own graph.} Samples are entity-indexed, and cross-department, cross-modal mentions of the same entity link to one identifier; the linking is measured against manifest ground truth, not assumed.
  \item \textbf{Erasable.} Because the corpus is a deterministic function of (inputs, view, engine version), deleting an entity regenerates the corpus with a byte-diff of exactly that entity's spans (the property we call the Amnesiac Corpus), and a sealed, seeded LoRA retrain recipe completes the erasure path from corpus to adapter.
\end{enumerate}

The erasure property is what the entity indexing buys; the entity indexing is what the standalone knowledge buys. Neither is the pitch on its own: the artifact exists so that training data can carry its own context, and the erasure machinery is the price of operating such a corpus on real enterprise data.

Entity indexing admits a further construction, which this artifact builds and receipts but does not claim benefit for: \emph{relational sample composition}. The per-block corpus is entity-\emph{indexed} but positionally \emph{segmented}: a sample is still one extracted block, the block being where text was found. The composed corpus replaces the segmentation rule with the graph: the builder traverses the resolved entity index and, for each entity, emits one training sample rendering all of that entity's evidence (a SQL row, an email signature, a transcript utterance, a deck bullet) as labeled source segments with a multi-source provenance list, under canonicalized, versioned ordering and cap policies. Cross-document facts become within-sample facts; sample boundaries follow knowledge structure rather than byte position. Its construction and erasure receipts are reported in Section~\ref{sec:composition}; its training benefit and its failure modes are the measured comparisons of Sections~\ref{sec:headtohead} and~\ref{sec:failuremodes}.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Positional chunking and its alternatives.}
Fixed-window chunking remains the default for document-to-training-data pipelines. GraphGen~\cite{chen2025graphgen} extracts a knowledge graph from source text and generates synthetic QA pairs from it, prioritizing long-tail knowledge. Our adjacency and our difference are both structural: GraphGen generates \emph{new synthetic} training samples from a graph, whereas we ground \emph{the actual samples} in the entity graph they mention. No text is generated, and the corpus remains a deterministic function of the input documents. That determinism is what makes the erasure property possible at all.

\paragraph{Cache-augmented generation.}
Cache-augmented generation (CAG)~\cite{chan2024cag} replaces question-time retrieval by preloading the knowledge base into a model's extended context, caching the runtime state, and answering queries with no lookup. Its evaluation uses corpora of 21k to 85k tokens, scores answers with BERTScore, and compares against sparse BM25 and dense retrieval baselines. The authors scope the method to knowledge of ``limited and manageable size'' that fits the extended context, and propose hybrid approaches that ``preload a foundation context and use retrieval only to augment edge cases or highly specific queries''~\cite{chan2024cag}. Adaptive contextual compression and a hybrid CAG-RAG design respond to that limit by compressing the preloaded context and adding selective retrieval when the preloaded context is insufficient~\cite{agrawal2025acc}. Our measured comparison is adjacent but not the same: it asks whether trained formats beat question-time retrieval over an entity graph, and it scores exact match, not BERTScore, on a 60-question estate; we do not replicate the CAG corpora, models, or baselines, so our numbers are not comparable to theirs, though the K7 arm that prefills this paper's composed records as a cache unit is the adjacent controlled comparison, scored exact match. Long-context studies bound the tradeoff from the other side: models use relevant information unevenly by position, with accuracy highest when the evidence sits at the beginning or end of the context and lower when it sits in the middle~\cite{liu2023lost}, and full-context answering can match or exceed retrieval when model capability and budget allow it, at substantially higher cost~\cite{li2024raglc}.

\paragraph{Entity-centric objectives versus composed units.}
The entity-pretraining line changes the \emph{objective}, not the training unit: Entities-as-Experts~\cite{fevry2020entities} attaches learned entity representations through sparse memory, and KEPLER~\cite{wang2019kepler} trains knowledge-embedding and language objectives jointly; both operate over public-encyclopedia text whose sample boundaries remain positional. The closest prior art to composition itself is in-context pretraining~\cite{shi2023incontext}, which reorders related documents into shared training contexts and reports downstream gains; but its grouping is retrieval-based similarity at pretraining scale, with no resolved identity, no per-sample provenance, and no erasure, whereas our composition groups by resolved entity and every composed byte carries a receipt. Multi-document composition exists as a training unit in summarization, but Multi-News~\cite{fabbri2019multinews} groups real documents by topic/event cluster with human-written targets, no resolved identity, and no provenance. The nearest structural neighbor to our composition, EmbGen~\cite{lenin2026embgen}, decomposes a corpus into entity-description pairs and reassembles them by embedding similarity to generate QA: synthetic output, embedding clusters in place of a resolved graph, no receipts. To our knowledge no prior artifact composes the actual source bytes of multiple heterogeneous documents into one deterministic training sample selected by traversing a resolved entity graph with per-sample provenance; the composition reported here keeps every rendered byte traceable to its source document and range.

\paragraph{Machine unlearning.}
SISA~\cite{bourtoule2020machine} limits a data point's influence by sharding the training set so that deletion requires retraining only the affected shard; approximate unlearning methods, benchmarked by TOFU~\cite{maini2024tofu}, modify weights without retraining but without receipts. Our mechanism is complementary and stricter in one axis: the corpus regeneration is exact and byte-verified, and the adapter path is a full deterministic retrain over the regenerated corpus (the sealed LoRA recipe~\cite{hu_lora}) rather than an approximate update. We do not claim approximate unlearning is unnecessary; we claim that a corpus which cannot be regenerated exactly cannot support any erasure receipt, approximate or otherwise.

\paragraph{Redaction is not a privacy guarantee.}
CLIOPATRA~\cite{annamalai2026cliopatra} shows layered redaction stacks failing under prompt injection, and LeakageBench~\cite{kumar2026leakagebench} shows that better localization does not make redacted documents release-safe. Carlini et al.~\cite{carlini_secret_sharer} and Lukas et al.~\cite{lukas_pii_leakage} establish that fine-tuned models memorize verbatim identifiers. We take these as constraints on what may be claimed: the gatekeeper certificate bounds residual \emph{detectable} PII at a stated confidence; it is not differential privacy, and the erasure claim for the adapter is completed only by the retrain, not by the masking.

\section{From Documents to Standalone Knowledge}
\label{sec:pipeline}

The pipeline runs entirely on-device. Perception is a stack of five zero-import WASM kernels (clean, SQL, graph, DL, media) exposed through a \texttt{perceiveMulti()} seam: SQL dumps, CSVs, plain text, email (\texttt{.eml}), meeting transcripts and captions (WebVTT), PCM16 audio (with prosody and speaker clustering on already-decoded PCM), and stored-ZIP decks (\texttt{.pptx}). Every detected value is vaulted behind a salted placeholder and assigned a stable entity identifier; detection, masking, and the conformal gate run inside the same sandbox, and the build emits a zero-egress receipt: $0$\,B egress, zero external calls, all nine network entry points replaced with throwing traps for the duration of the run.

Three properties of the corpus follow from the seam design:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Determinism.} The corpus is a pure function of (inputs, view, engine version): no timestamps, no unseeded randomness, fixed key order, content-digest sample IDs and ordering. A receipt pins the corpus SHA-256 and the engine-version hash, so kernel or source drift invalidates rather than silently corrupts.
  \item \textbf{Entity indexing.} Every sample records the entity identifiers it mentions; the entity index records every sample an entity touches. A ground-truth person (name, email, phone) is three values and therefore three vault identifiers; the corpus indexes each value, and cross-modal spoken-form variants resolve onto the same salted identifiers.
  \item \textbf{Certificates.} Each sample is re-scanned after masking; any residual scanner hit that is not a vault placeholder counts against the sample. The per-sample certificate is the conformal gate's decision with a certified binomial recall bound (Clopper--Pearson, fixed-sequence Learn-Then-Test over the shipped binomial CDF kernel).
\end{itemize}

\section{The Sample Schema}
\label{sec:schema}

A training sample in the exported JSONL is:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{id}: SHA-256 content digest over source, byte-range (or a derived-format marker), and text; byte-range collisions fail loudly as engine bugs, and benign repeated derived-format content (a quoted line, two same-titled slides) disambiguates deterministically.
  \item \texttt{source}, \texttt{sourceRange}: provenance, the input name and, on plain-byte surfaces (txt/csv/sql), exact byte ranges into the source. Derived formats (eml/pptx/vtt/wav) reflow text, so those blocks carry no byte range; this is recorded, not smoothed.
  \item \texttt{entities}: the vault-salted entity identifiers the sample mentions. In the per-block export this field is JSONL metadata; the training loss sees identity only where a view renders it into tokens (the composed corpus names its target entity, and the identity-pair set renders ids as the SFT answer).
  \item \texttt{messages}: the SFT turn structure (system prompt, user task, masked assistant content), with placeholder masking so the retrain recipe can mask loss on vault spans.
\end{itemize}

Per-sample certificates ride in the run artifact keyed by the same content-digest ID (adding fields to the JSONL lines would change the frozen corpus SHA-256 receipts, so the seam bytes are reused untouched).

The same seam exposes two declarative view dimensions on top of this schema. \texttt{identity}: the \texttt{vaulted} default described above, or an explicit \texttt{readable} opt-in for the user training a model on their own documents who wants it to learn real names: unmasked content and canonical readable entity anchors, with no extra ceremony. The vaulted machinery is not a tier or an upsell; it is simply how the engine runs by default. \texttt{composition}: the per-block corpus above, or the composed corpus of Section~\ref{sec:composition}. Both dimensions are deterministic pure functions of (inputs, view, engine version), erasure is span-exact in every view, and the view is recorded in the receipt. Which claim lives where matters: in the vaulted default the retrain recipe masks loss on vault spans, so the adapter learns structure, not who anyone is; the standalone-knowledge \emph{training} benefit, if it exists, lives in the readable view, and the designed comparison of Section~\ref{sec:claims} therefore runs readable by default. The vaulted default's own claim is lineage and exact erasure, not learned names.

\paragraph{Detection and certificates.}
Over the estate, the scanner detects with zero misses: PERSON $153$, EMAIL $57$, PHONE $28$, DATE\_OF\_BIRTH $20$, ADDRESS $3$, and one injected GITHUB\_PAT, which quarantines its sample by design. Organizational IDs and project codenames have no engine detector class; they are estate ground truth only, and no coverage is claimed for them. The per-sample certificate pass rate is $95.8333\%$ (23 of 24 samples), with a certified lower bound of $81.7108\%$ at stated $95\%$. The certificate is detector-closed: it re-scans with the same detector family that performed the masking, so it certifies absence of PII detectable by this scanner, not absence to an independent auditor; and with 24 samples the lower bound is small-sample arithmetic, not evidence.

\paragraph{Cross-department linking.}
The manifest's ground truth defines 54 (entity, department-pair) cross-department links; the pipeline recovers all 54 with zero false positives (precision, recall, and F1 at $100\%$ at that unit), and all 16 ground-truth people resolve to the same vault identifiers on the IT and business sides. The emitted connection graph has 92 nodes and 208 edges over the masked corpus, SHA-pinned in the artifact. This is the standalone-knowledge property measured: records in different departments, in different modalities, about the same person, carry the same identity into training. The unit linked is the value, not the person: a person's name, email, and phone carry separate identifiers, and 54/54 recovery at this unit is exact matching on planted values. Noise-tolerant resolution (nicknames, shared inboxes, same-name collisions, stale records), scored with pairwise and B-cubed precision and recall including false-merge rate, is the open measurement of the benchmark line: the noisy estate built for it (Section~\ref{sec:headtohead}) drops the trivial exact-match baseline to a $24.27\%$ pairwise recall floor, and the openly licensed second estate of Section~\ref{sec:openestate} holds the same machinery under a $61.46\%$ floor on real meeting text. The estate's own PPRL benchmark measures a $4.88\%$ false-link rate at its dev threshold on a 500-common-names collision set, and that is the regime real estates live in.

\section{Relational Composition: Built, Receipted, Measured}
\label{sec:composition}

The composed corpus is emitted by the same builder under a versioned composition policy (receipted as \texttt{composed-v1}). For each resolved entity, one SFT sample renders all of that entity's evidence blocks as labeled \texttt{[source]} segments in canonical order (byte-ranged blocks first, ordered by source name and byte offset, then derived-format blocks) under a fixed per-entity block cap, so high-degree entities cannot flood the corpus with near-duplicate renders. Every composed sample names its target entity and carries a provenance array enumerating each contributing document and its range.

Three receipts hold today and are asserted in CI. \emph{Determinism}: two composed builds over the same inputs are byte-identical. \emph{Erasure re-proven on the composed corpus, not inherited}: erasing the entity a composed sample exists to render retracts that sample from the corpus, while erasing a co-mentioned entity removes exactly that entity's spans from the render; and a build-failing guard asserts that the two internal derivations of the same composed erasure agree byte-for-byte, so a subtle divergence fails the build instead of shipping. \emph{Regression}: the default per-block corpus is untouched by the composition code path; its pinned corpus SHA-256 reproduces.

The measurement exists now. Whether an adapter trained on composed samples beats, matches, or loses to per-block, flat-chunked, or retrieval baselines is the head-to-head of Section~\ref{sec:headtohead}, and composition carries two measured costs alongside its benefit: the false-merge amplification and hub duplication of Section~\ref{sec:failuremodes}.

\subsection{Failure Modes, Measured}
\label{sec:failuremodes}

Composition ships with two costs the per-block corpus does not carry, and both are measured on the noisy estate rather than designed.

\paragraph{False-merge amplification.}
Inducing controlled false merges over a ladder of merge rates (the 20 designed collision pairs always merged, plus a seeded prefix of disjoint random pairs, 0 to 72 merged person classes) and measuring corruption as person occurrences carried in a record that mixes two real people under one identity: at a $25\%$ merge rate the composed corpus corrupts $0.3214$ of its world-mappable occurrences against $0.0224$ for per-block, an amplification factor of $14.32$; at $50\%$ the rates are $0.5512$ against $0.0431$, a factor of $12.79$. At the two lowest rates ($5\%$ and $10\%$) per-block corruption is exactly zero while composed already sits at $0.1329$ and $0.1786$; the amplification factors there are reported as null, not smoothed over. Per-block is not undamaged: every falsely merged mention is attributed to a false identity in both views ($148$ to $614$ occurrences across the ladder); composition's additional damage is that one record renders both people under one canonical anchor, and all 72 merged classes produce such a false-name anchor at the top rate.

\paragraph{Hub duplication.}
MinHash near-duplicate analysis of the composed corpus ($1{,}113$ samples, 16-character shingles, 128 hash functions, 32 bands of 4 rows, candidate pairs confirmed by exact shingle Jaccard $\geq 0.8$): $20{,}627$ near-duplicate pairs of which $12{,}569$ are exact duplicates, and every composed sample sits in a near-duplicate pair (fraction $1.0$). The mechanism is co-mention block reuse: one evidence block renders inside the record of every co-mentioning entity, so the corpus repeats its own source bytes. The six samples truncated by the hub cap are the near-duplicate-free ones, so the cap policy is not the cause; co-mention is. The degree-versus-benefit saturation curve (whether a $k$-degree entity's record still helps at $k \geq 8$) stays designed-not-run, recorded in the artifact with its full design.

\section{The Six-Arm Head-to-Head, Measured}
\label{sec:headtohead}

The comparison this artifact was built for: same estate, same token budget per arm, same recipe, and the answer recorded whether or not it flatters the design.

\paragraph{The estate.}
The noisy estate is deterministic (seed 20261124): $115$ documents over five text modalities (rosters in CSV and SQL, memos, chat logs, meeting captions), $250$ people, $2{,}368$ person mentions, and the eight designed noise types of the spec (typos and OCR damage, nicknames, initials, case and format variants, shared inboxes, 20 same-name collision pairs, stale records, cross-document disagreement). The trivial exact-match baseline over identically normalized mention surfaces scores pairwise recall $24.27\%$ at precision $91.83\%$ with $257$ false merges, every one on the designed collision pairs; the binding gate (recall under $90\%$ plus at least one collision false merge) passes, so the estate is a benchmark and not a solved key. Sixty eval questions live only in the manifest: $24$ single-document (Q1), $24$ cross-document (Q2), and $12$ disambiguation questions between same-name people (Q3). The engine and every training corpus see only the estate documents; the questions, answers, and truth never enter any arm's training data.

\paragraph{The design.}
Five trained arms at one budget of $25{,}755$ training tokens each (prompt plus target after the assistant-loss render): flat 800-character windows in document order; the per-block readable corpus; the composed corpus; the composed corpus plus identity-contrastive pairs; and the per-block vaulted corpus, the privacy-cost ablation of the per-block arm. Two arms train nothing: Graph-PPR retrieval (the personalized-page-rank index over the same entity graph, top-6 contexts fed to the untrained base model at question time) and the base model alone. The recipe is sealed from a prior 18-combination epoch and learning-rate sweep: Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, LoRA rank 16, $\alpha$ 32, 160 steps, learning rate $2 \times 10^{-4}$; a 104-row canary batch (12 canaries at 1, 5, and 20 repetitions) is appended identically to every trained arm, so canary exposure is comparable across arms. Answers are scored by exact match against manifest values, plus two blinded judges that never see the arm identity: a strict Qwen2.5-7B judge and a cross-family generous Llama-3.2-3B judge, whose row agreement ($6.7$ to $41.7\%$ across arms) is recorded because the judges disagree and neither is cherry-picked. Such sensitivity is documented: LLM judges carry positional, verbosity, and self-preference biases~\cite{zheng2023judging}, and their rankings can be manipulated by changing response order~\cite{wang2023fair}.

\end{table}

\paragraph{The reading, recorded as it ran.}
Four results carry weight. First, the graph pays off as a retrieval index, not as a training format, at this scale: retrieval with no fine-tuning answers $53.3\%$ exactly, $4.6$ times the best trained arm, and $83.3\%$ on the cross-document stratum the composition policy was designed for, because the contexts it retrieves already join the documents the trained arms must bake into weights. The win is a retrieval result, not a long-context result: it supplies six retrieved contexts to a 1.5B base, below the regime in which long-context answering has been reported to match or exceed retrieval~\cite{li2024raglc}. Second, among the trained formats the design's ordering holds on every stratum: composed ($11.7\%$) over per-block ($10.0\%$) over flat ($6.7\%$), with composed also best on the disambiguation stratum ($16.7\%$); the margins are small, and 60 questions make every cell a handful of items. Third, the composed-plus-pairs arm failed by mechanism, not by verdict on the pair idea: under the shared budget the pair rows displaced composed rows until only $50$ rows with $9{,}947$ learnable tokens survived (against $41$ rows and $23{,}608$ learnable tokens for composed alone), and the arm scores exactly base. Fourth, the vaulted ablation scores base on closed-book identity questions ($1.7\%$) because the vault masks $36.31\%$ of subwords: the identity the questions ask about was never in the learnable text. That is the privacy cost quantified, and it is why the readable opt-in seam exists for the user training a model on their own documents.

\paragraph{Non-training controls.}
Three further arms train nothing and were scored by exact match only, with no judges run, on the same estate, rows, and base model as the K3 arms. A from-scratch BM25 retriever over the same passage pool (Okapi BM25, $k_1{=}1.5$, $b{=}0.75$, $216$ passages reconstructed from the raw estate bytes), feeding top-6 contexts to the base model, answers $60.0\%$ overall ($58.3\%$ Q1, $87.5\%$ Q2, $8.3\%$ Q3), which ties or edges the graph retriever's $53.3\%$ within noise at $n{=}60$; on this estate the graph's contribution is not retrieval quality. The per-stratum counts show a direction rather than an inferential claim, at stratum sizes of $24$, $24$, and $12$: BM25 answers $1$ of $12$ disambiguation questions correctly, Graph-PPR $2$ of $12$, the cache arm $4$ of $12$, and the hybrid routing arm $9$ of $12$. Cache-augmented generation over the composed records prefills the cache once, $8$ composed per-entity records selected by greedy set-cover over the $52$ unique question persons and $16607$ prefill tokens, inside the corpus-size regime the CAG literature evaluates~\cite{chan2024cag}; it ties the retrieval arms overall, within noise at $n{=}60$ ($51.7\%$ against $53.3\%$ and $60.0\%$), leads on disambiguation ($33.3\%$, $4$ of $12$), and trails on single-document questions ($37.5\%$ against BM25's $58.3\%$). The token accounting is the amortization: each question appends $15$ mean tokens after the one-time prefill, against $1004$ mean prompt tokens per question for the retrieval arms. The mechanism is visible in the strata: a fixed cache cannot fetch the one document that holds the answer, while the composed records carry enough cross-document context to help the collision questions. A third arm measures the per-question selection this suggests, the hybrid direction the CAG literature itself proposes~\cite{chan2024cag}: per question, the records whose own target carries the asked person's canonical identity key (the person's $2$ or $3$ subject records) are prefilled as a fresh cache, and the question is appended to it. It answers $81.7\%$ overall ($87.5\%$ Q1, $79.2\%$ Q2, $75.0\%$ Q3, $9$ of $12$ disambiguation), the strongest arm in the table; at $n{=}60$ this is one estate's measurement. Its router receives the question's resolved person identity instead of doing retrieval, so disambiguation is settled by routing rather than in context; in deployment that resolution is the identity graph's own task and the questions state the full name, but the arm scores routing given a correct resolution, not resolution itself. The gain gives up the amortization: a mean of $2940$ prefill tokens rebuilds the cache per question, against the fixed cache's one-time $16607$-token prefill, though each question still appends only $15$ mean tokens. Cache invalidation at erasure granularity follows from the byte-diff receipts the corpus machinery already produces, a mechanism this measurement does not benchmark.

\paragraph{Uncertainty at $n{=}60$.}
Every exact-match percentage in Table~\ref{tab:k3} carries an exact binomial (Clopper--Pearson) $95\%$ interval, and at $n{=}60$ those intervals are wide: the retrieval and cache arms are statistically indistinguishable from one another (Graph-PPR $53.3\%$, interval $40.0$ to $66.3$; BM25 $60.0\%$, $46.5$ to $72.4$; CAG $51.7\%$, $38.4$ to $64.8$), the hybrid routing arm's $81.7\%$ ($69.6$ to $90.5$) clears the graph retriever's and cache arm's upper bounds while overlapping only the top of BM25's, and the trained arms are separated from every retrieval arm, with the best trained format at $11.7\%$ ($4.8$ to $22.6$) against $10.0\%$ ($3.8$ to $20.5$) per-block, $6.7\%$ ($1.8$ to $16.2$) flat, and $1.7\%$ ($0.0$ to $8.9$) for the untrained base.

\paragraph{Honesty boundaries of this measurement.}
Canary exposure is flat across trained arms ($8.48$ to $8.57$ bits over the 12 canaries, against $4.25$ bits for the untrained base): the training-format choice does not move memorization at this scale. Held-out perplexity degrades slightly for every trained arm (composed at $32.5$ against base at $26.1$) and worst for vaulted ($43.9$). The regime is capacity-starved by design: a 1.5B adapter, 160 steps, $25{,}755$ tokens, each estate fact appearing in one or two documents, where knowledge-capacity results~\cite{allenzhu2024knowledge} predict that facts seen in few varied forms are barely extractable. Whether the composed-over-flat ordering grows, holds, or inverts at larger budgets is open work; the retrieval arm's win is the one result with no training-budget dependence.

\section{External Validity: A Second Estate from Openly Licensed Data}
\label{sec:openestate}

\paragraph{A layered estate, never mixed.}
The noisy estate is synthetic, and this paper says so. The second estate is built from openly licensed real data under one rule: real identity and overlay identity are scored in separate layers, and no metric mixes them. Layer R is real: 12 meeting transcripts from AMI and ICSI as distributed by QMSum (MIT repackaging; the underlying corpus is CC BY 4.0), truth = the corpus's own speaker labels, plus six pinned AMI Mix-Headset recordings (CC BY 4.0, per-file SHA-256 pins) whose 62 corpus-labeled people are keyed exactly as the corpus distributes them; overlay never touches audio. Layer O plants a 60-person overlay cast over real transcript text with the same eight noise types as the noisy estate, truth by construction. Every fixture is committed as data with its license recorded in the manifest.

\paragraph{The floor on real data.}
The same trivial exact-match machinery, applied per layer. Layer O: pairwise recall $61.46\%$, precision $76.10\%$, and $2{,}303$ false merges, every one on the 12 designed collision pairs; the binding gate passes, so overlay identity over real text is a benchmark and not a solved key. Layer R is reported as-is and ungated: recall $100\%$ at precision $22.79\%$, because exact-match transitive closure over identically normalized surfaces collapses nothing by design there (corpus role labels repeat verbatim across meetings); that is the honest empty-achievement calibration number, not an engine score. As a third, conditional slice, an Enron email subset (no stated license; benchmarked on the authors' machine only, with zero Enron bytes in this repository and aggregates plus an archive SHA-256 pin recorded in the artifact) floors at pairwise recall $26.60\%$ and precision $82.06\%$, with $273$ false merges of which $260$ fall on designed collision pairs. These numbers are floors for engine claims on real data, not engine claims: identity resolution on this estate is the open Stage K5 measurement.

\section{The Erasure Property}
\label{sec:erasure}

Under GDPR Article~17~\cite{gdpr_art17}, erasure of personal data must extend to models trained on it; a corpus that is deleted but a model that is not is not erasure. The Amnesiac Corpus bundles both halves:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Corpus half (measured above).} \texttt{eraseEntity} regenerates the corpus minus one entity with a diff receipt: changed samples, removed span count and bytes, corpus SHA-256 before and after, and a failure that throws, loudly, whenever the byte-diff exceeds the erased entity's spans. Receipts are version-pinned: an engine-version drift invalidates regeneration instead of silently producing different bytes.
  \item \textbf{Adapter half (pending GPU backfill).} The export ships a sealed, seeded LoRA retrain recipe (rank 16, $\alpha$ 32, fixed steps and learning rate, placeholder-masked loss on vault spans); erasure completes by retraining the adapter over the regenerated corpus. The three GPU-dependent measurements (retrain wall-clock versus full retrain from scratch, a head-to-head against SISA on the same estate, and Carlini canary exposure~\cite{carlini_secret_sharer} pre- versus post-retrain against the chance rate) are structured in the artifact as \texttt{pending-gpu} and appear in this paper as measured numbers only when they are measured. No placeholder values are shown.
\end{itemize}

\section{What This Paper Does Not Claim}
\label{sec:claims}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{No differential privacy, no anonymity.} The gatekeeper certificate bounds residual detectable PII at a stated confidence, is detector-closed, and is small-sample (Section~\ref{sec:measurements}); it is a build-time check, not an audit. CLIOPATRA- and LeakageBench-class results are precisely why redaction is not promoted to a privacy guarantee here.
  \item \textbf{No generalization claim.} The perception and linking numbers are construction-validation on synthetic, ground-truth-by-construction fixtures. They prove the pipeline does what it is constructed to do on data whose truth is known; they do not predict open-world accuracy. The second estate (Section~\ref{sec:openestate}) does not change that: it validates the floor machinery on openly licensed real data, not the engine.
  \item \textbf{The downstream claim, measured at one scale, with its boundaries stated.} The motivating hypothesis, that training on graph-grounded, standalone-knowledge samples beats training on positionally stitched chunks, is measured in Section~\ref{sec:headtohead}: the composed corpus wins over per-block and flat formats on every stratum, but by small margins ($11.7\%$ versus $10.0\%$ and $6.7\%$ closed-book exact match at the equal budget), and the same entity graph used as a retrieval index with no fine-tuning beats every trained arm $4.6$ times over ($53.3\%$). The boundaries of that measurement are the claim: one 1.5B-parameter adapter, 160 steps, a $25{,}755$-token budget, and one synthetic noisy estate; knowledge-capacity results~\cite{allenzhu2024knowledge} say this is a repetition-starved regime, so behavior at larger budgets is open work, as is the Stage K5 resolution-scoring line (pairwise and B-cubed by surface-form difficulty on the noisy and open estates) and the degree-versus-benefit saturation curve, which stays designed-not-run. Two honesty measurements ran with the head-to-head and now bound every composition claim (Section~\ref{sec:failuremodes}): false-merge amplification (a factor of $14.32$ at a $25\%$ merge rate) and hub duplication (a $1.0$ near-duplicate fraction from co-mention reuse). Composition is therefore a property with receipts, a small measured benefit at the scale measured, and measured costs attached.
  \item \textbf{No approximate-unlearning replacement claim.} The retrain path is the honest completion of erasure; whether approximate methods suffice for a given tolerance is a policy decision the receipts inform but do not make.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The artifact this paper reports takes a stand on what training data should look like: not windows of positions, but records that know which entities they describe, where their bytes came from, that they are clean at a stated confidence, and that they can be removed exactly when the person they describe asks. The motivating comparison is no longer the next experiment: it is measured, and its answer is more useful than the hypothesis. At a 1.5B-parameter, capacity-starved scale, the entity graph pays off as a retrieval index ($53.3\%$ closed-book exact match against $11.7\%$ for the best trained format, $83.3\%$ on cross-document questions) and, in the strongest measured arm, as a router ($81.7\%$ when each question's cache is prefilled from the asked person's own records, against $60.0\%$ for the lexical control), composition wins over per-block and flat formats on every stratum by small margins, and the composed corpus carries measured costs alongside the benefit: false merges amplify by a factor of $14.32$, and co-mention reuse makes near-duplicates of the whole corpus. The honest reading closes the thesis this artifact was built to test: records that know what they describe are worth building for erasure, provenance, and retrieval, their training-format benefit is real and must be claimed at the scale it was measured, and their failure modes travel with the claim.
