\begin{abstract}
Enterprise AI corpora span leak-prone containers---emails (\texttt{.eml}, \texttt{.mbox}, Outlook \texttt{.msg}), Office files with hidden revisions or caches (\texttt{.docx} \texttt{<w:delText>}, \texttt{.pptx} hidden slides and speaker notes, \texttt{.xlsx} \texttt{veryHidden} sheets and \texttt{pivotCache}), DevOps artifacts (\texttt{.har}, \texttt{.env}, Kubernetes Secrets), call audio (\texttt{.wav}), and video (\texttt{.mp4}). Rather than claiming a new neural architecture, \textbf{Versieve-MM} makes a systems-integration and conformal-gating contribution: consolidating container parsing, archive defense, Unicode anti-evasion, secret scanning, Bloom-filter record linkage, spoken inverse text normalization (ITN), and audio/pixel redaction inside a single $187{,}087$\,B Rust-to-WebAssembly kernel (\texttt{verisieve\_media.wasm}, $24$ exports, \textbf{zero host imports}) shared byte-identically across Node.js CLI/MCP and the live RTIO browser workbench, coupled with human-calibrated Learn-Then-Test (LTT) release gating. We evaluate Versieve-MM against strong third-party baselines across real human-annotated legal rulings, external secret/code corpora, and locally synthesized spoken-audio tests. On the Text Anonymization Benchmark (\textbf{TAB}, $127$ held-out ECHR court rulings), the $8.7\times$ larger Python server baseline Microsoft Presidio (\texttt{en\_core\_web\_lg}, $400.7$\,MB) supplemented with the same 1-line ECHR case-number regex wins decisively on F1 ($79.26\%$ All-Maskable, $86.27\%$ Person, $98.08\%$ \texttt{DIRECT} recall). Compact $45.8$\,MB INT8 \texttt{gliner-pii-edge-v1.0} filtered with $231$ \texttt{NO\_MASK} forms learned strictly from \texttt{echr\_dev} improves Person F1 by $+28.65$ over Paper~1 regex ($74.20\%$ vs.\ $45.55\%$), while honestly lowering All-Maskable F1 relative to deterministic Stage~3A+Code ($45.91\%$ vs.\ $51.67\%$) due to legal-prose false positives. On TAB's $1{,}999$ held-out paragraphs ($81.2\%$ human-labeled unsafe, $18.8\%$ safe), comparing three human-calibrated LTT gates shows that a \textbf{Detector-Informed Hybrid LTT gate} achieves $0.55\%$ Conditional FPR at $\alpha{=}0.01$ with $6.90\%$ overall auto-release ($34.31\%$ Safe Yield, $6.52\%$ residual leak among released)---$2.2\times$ the Safe Yield of an NER-Max-Confidence LTT baseline ($15.43\%$, $1.17\%$ FPR)---and $4.62\%$ FPR at $\alpha{=}0.05$ ($12.91\%$ auto-release, $48.67\%$ Safe Yield). On $520$ external secret/code files (\texttt{pstu-synthetic-secrets} + Yelp \texttt{test\_data} + $374$ \texttt{MBPP} Python scripts), steady-state $10\times$ throughput (excluding process startup) places \texttt{verisieve\_media.wasm} ($56.55\%$ recall, $72.25\%$ F1, $0\%$ clean FP) at $2.97$\,MB/s---$4.5\times$ faster than \texttt{Gitleaks v8.30.1} ($0.66$\,MB/s, $66.36\%$ F1) and $22.9\times$ faster than Yelp \texttt{detect-secrets} ($0.13$\,MB/s, $86.27\%$ F1). Across $25$ offline-synthesized spoken calls ($97$ planted PII tokens) evaluated by a $3.3\times$ larger cross-model attacker (\texttt{faster-whisper small.en} vs.\ defender \texttt{base.en}), PII leakage on the same planted-token denominator drops from $94.85\%$ to $20.62\%$ ($-74.23$\,pp; $15/25$ calls zero leak; $100\%$ non-PII speech retained). Finally, a zero-import $128$-bit salted Bloom+Soundex linkage signature matches plaintext Jaro-Winkler on $40$ held-out noisy pairs ($83.33\%$ F1) while revealing the scale collision trade-off across $124{,}750$ common-name pairs ($4.88\%$ FP at $\tau{=}0.60$ vs.\ $0.27\%$ FP at $\tau{=}0.75$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Regulated clinical, legal, and enterprise AI workflows cannot transmit raw documents, call recordings, or DevOps traces to cloud de-identification endpoints. Our companion study of \textbf{Versieve} \citep{ardiyansyah2026verisieve} introduced zero-import WebAssembly text cleaning, Dual-CSR knowledge graphs, and Learn-Then-Test (LTT) conformal gatekeeping \citep{angelopoulos2021ltt}. Scaling that philosophy to heterogeneous enterprise records exposes two core requirements: (i)~a load-time auditable capability boundary (\texttt{WebAssembly.Module.imports(mod).length === 0} \citep{haas2017wasm}) covering archives, secrets, audio, and pixels across Node.js and browsers; and (ii)~honest evaluation against strong third-party baselines without cherry-picked operating points, label circularity, or process-startup timing artifacts.

\textbf{Scope of Novelty.} Individually, DEFLATE inflation, LTT calibration, GLiNER \citep{zaratiana2024gliner}, Bloom-filter record linkage \citep{schnell2009pprl}, and regex-plus-NER fusion \citep{presidio2021} are well-established methods. Versieve-MM makes two concrete contributions: (1)~a \emph{systems-integration contribution} consolidating seven multimodal sanitization kernels into one $187{,}087$\,B zero-import Rust-to-WASM binary (\texttt{verisieve\_media.wasm}, $24$ exports) running byte-identically in Node/MCP and the live RTIO browser sandbox (Figure~\ref{fig:mm-arch}); and (2)~to our knowledge, the first empirical evaluation of \emph{detector-informed, human-calibrated LTT release gating} on the Text Anonymization Benchmark (TAB) \citep{pilan2022tab}, measuring Conditional FPR, overall automation rate, Safe Yield, and end-to-end residual leakage alongside a direct NER-confidence LTT baseline.

\subsection{Live RTIO Browser Sandbox Integration}
In the companion RTIO Next.js workbench (\url{https://www.rtio.io/sandbox}), \texttt{verisieve\_media.wasm} executes live on every upload: \texttt{maskTextContextAware} fuses Paper~1 regexes with Stage~3A WASM secret/anti-evasion scanning; \texttt{CleanTab} displays explicit forensic leak counts (DOCX \texttt{<w:delText>}, PPTX hidden slides/notes, XLSX \texttt{veryHidden}/\texttt{pivotCache}, HAR credentials, K8s Base64 Secrets, RIFF/MP4 atoms); multi-gigabyte \texttt{.mp4}/\texttt{.mov} files run $O(1)$ \texttt{Blob.slice()} ISOBMFF atom audits without loading \texttt{mdat} into RAM; and an interactive \texttt{MediaRedactionStudio} performs client-side WAV silence/beep redaction (with before/after playback and \texttt{.wav} download) plus video/image canvas blackout/pixelate/blur (with \texttt{.png} download). For browser audio and video drops, an off-main-thread ONNX Runtime Web proxy Worker (\texttt{/ort/ort-wasm-simd-threaded.*.wasm}) executes quantized multilingual \texttt{whisper-tiny} ($40.8$\,MB \texttt{q8}, cached in browser CacheStorage) over DSP Voice Activity Detection (VAD) $30$\,s active speech windows alongside $1080$p dark-mode contrast-inverted keyframe slide OCR (\texttt{/ocr/*}) and timestamp-agnostic speech deduplication; Stage~3B neural ONNX inference (\texttt{gliner-pii-edge}) executes in local Node/Python runtimes and fails closed (\texttt{NER\_WEIGHTS\_REQUIRED}) when local weights are absent.

Removing process-startup bias reduces our speedup over \texttt{Gitleaks v8.30.1} from an inflated $14.6\times$ (cold) to a true steady-state \textbf{$4.5\times$} ($2.97$ vs.\ $0.66$\,MB/s; $25.55$\,ms even including Node CLI JSON I/O) and \textbf{$22.9\times$} over Yelp \texttt{detect-secrets} ($0.13$\,MB/s). \subsection{Exp.~4: 25-Call End-to-End Audio PII Redaction Against a Stronger Cross-Model Attacker}
\label{sec:exp4}

We synthesize $25$ realistic customer-service calls locally via offline Windows SAPI TTS ($97$ planted PII tokens across names, spoken emails, and spoken $10$-digit phones). The defender transcribes with \texttt{faster-whisper base.en} (word timestamps), runs \texttt{verisieve\_media.wasm} Spoken ITN + Stage~3A/3B GLiNER-Edge, and silences matched spans ($60$\,ms padding, $5$\,ms cosine fade). To avoid same-model bias, a \textbf{$3.3\times$ larger cross-model attacker} (\texttt{faster-whisper small.en}) evaluates both raw and redacted WAVs on the **same planted-token denominator ($n{=}97$)**:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pre-Redaction Exposure:} $92/97 = \mathbf{94.85\%}$ (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[88.5\%, 97.8\%]$).
    \item \textbf{Post-Redaction Leakage:} $20/97 = \mathbf{20.62\%}$ (Wilson $95\%$ CI $[13.8\%, 29.7\%]$)---an exact \textbf{$-74.23$\,pp absolute drop} ($78.26\%$ relative reduction of exposed tokens, $15/25$ calls zero residual leak) with \textbf{$100.0\%$ non-PII business speech preserved} (Figure~\ref{fig:e3}A).
\end{itemize}
The remaining $20.62\%$ residual leakage arises primarily from \texttt{base.en} fusing spoken emails (\texttt{Elena.vansatacme.org}) or splitting dots, which the stronger \texttt{small.en} attacker still recovers if left unmasked.

\subsection{Exp.~5: PPRL Reality Check (Plaintext Ceilings, Scale Collisions) and Kernel Sanity}
\label{sec:exp5}

To avoid overfitting small hand-crafted pairs, we tune thresholds strictly on a disjoint $20$-pair Dev set ($\tau_{\text{Dice}}{=}0.60$) and evaluate on $40$ held-out Test pairs ($20$ OCR/ASR noisy + $20$ hard same-first-name negatives) against **plaintext non-private ceilings** (Jaro-Winkler and normalized Levenshtein). On held-out Test, Exact HMAC scores $0.0\%$ F1, Plaintext Levenshtein reaches $85.11\%$ F1 ($74.1\%$ P / $100\%$ R), Plaintext Jaro-Winkler reaches $83.33\%$ F1 ($71.4\%$ P / $100\%$ R), and zero-import WASM Salted Bloom+Soundex PPRL matches Jaro-Winkler at \textbf{$83.33\%$ F1} ($71.4\%$ P / $100\%$ R) (Figure~\ref{fig:e3}B).

\paragraph{Scale Collision Test ($500$ Common Names $= 124{,}750$ Negative Pairs).}
Evaluating all $500 \times 499 / 2 = 124{,}750$ distinct full-name pairs ($25$ common first $\times$ $20$ common last names) demonstrates why permissive thresholds cannot be deployed at scale: at $\tau{=}0.60$, pairwise false links reach \textbf{$4.88\%$ ($6{,}090/124{,}750$)}, whereas tightening to $\tau{=}0.75$ lowers false links $18\times$ to \textbf{$0.270\%$ ($337/124{,}750$)}---$3.7\times$ fewer collisions than Plaintext Jaro-Winkler ($1.007\%$, $1{,}256/124{,}750$). Furthermore, we explicitly caution against over-claiming cryptographic privacy for $128$-bit Bloom filters: small Bloom encodings are vulnerable to frequency-analysis cryptanalysis when an attacker has auxiliary population lists \citep{christen2018bloomattack}, and Soundex is English-centric; thus PPRL in Versieve-MM should be treated as a \emph{local-only noisy erasure index}, never a safe external export format.

\paragraph{Graph-Coupled Cross-Modal Conformal Redaction (\texttt{GC-CRC}).}
To resolve the \emph{fragmented context horizon} of chunk-isolated detectors---where an entity introduced with clear context on Page~1 of a ruling or in an email (\emph{``Mr.\ Markus Pentik\"ainen''}) reappears as an unprefixed surname, ASR homophone (\emph{``Pentikaynen''}), or OCR substitution (\emph{``Pentikalnen''}) in subsequent chunks where local confidence falls below threshold ($s_{\text{local}} < \tau$)---Versieve introduces \textbf{\texttt{GC-CRC}} (\texttt{src/perceive.js}). Analogous to sparse reference pools in long-horizon document perception, \texttt{GC-CRC} seeds a bounded Salted Reference Anchor Pool $A = \{(e_k, \text{sig}_k, v_k)\}$ ($|A| \le 512$) from high-precision spans ($s_{\text{local}} \ge \tau_{\text{anchor}}{=}0.60$) using $128$-bit WASM \texttt{vs\_media\_pprl\_signature} encodings, then propagates anchor risk to candidate tokens across all document, audio ASR, and video OCR blocks via:
\begin{equation}
S_{\text{GC}}(m, c) = 1 - \bigl(1 - s_{\text{local}}(m)\bigr)\bigl(1 - \gamma \max_{a \in A} \operatorname{Sim}(\text{sig}(m), \text{sig}_a)(1 + \beta d_{a,c}^{-1})\bigr).
\end{equation}
Across a $60$-case, $240$-block ($4$-modality) cross-modal benchmark (\texttt{scripts/eval-gccrc-benchmark.mjs}), isolated high-precision hybrid filtering ($\tau{=}0.60$) misses $100\%$ of weak cross-modal follow-ups ($34.72\%$ overall recall, $62.50\%$ precision, $44.64\%$ F1), while lowering the isolated threshold to $\tau{=}0.20$ inflates domain false positives by $+140\%$ ($75 \to 180$ FPs, $62.89\%$ precision, $72.19\%$ F1). By contrast, \textbf{\texttt{GC-CRC}} ($\tau_{\text{anchor}}{=}0.60$) recovers \textbf{$76.11\%$} of noisy cross-modal follow-ups ($83.03\%$ consistent entity-link rate) while adding \textbf{zero} extra domain false positives ($75$ FPs, \textbf{$77.74\%$ precision}), achieving the highest overall \textbf{$75.18\%$ F1} ($+30.54$\,F1 over isolated $\tau{=}0.60$ and $+14.85$\,pp higher precision than isolated $\tau{=}0.20$).

\section{Limitations and Operating Boundaries}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Edge vs.\ Server NER Gap:} On TAB legal rulings, Microsoft Presidio (\texttt{en\_core\_web\_lg} + ECHR code regex, $400.7$\,MB) outperforms our $45.8$\,MB edge GLiNER model by $+33.35$\,F1 on All-Maskable entities and $+12.07$\,F1 on \texttt{PERSON}, and adding $45.8$\,MB GLiNER to Stage~3A+Code lowers All-Maskable F1 ($51.67\% \to 45.91\%$).
    \item \textbf{Modest Overall Gate Automation \& Exchangeability:} On PII-dense TAB rulings ($81.2\%$ unsafe), Detector-Hybrid LTT auto-releases $6.9\%$--$12.9\%$ of all paragraphs at $\alpha \in \{0.01, 0.05\}$; intra-document paragraph correlation also causes slight finite-sample FPR overshoots at looser budgets ($\alpha{=}0.10$), motivating document-clustered LTT calibration in future work.
    \item \textbf{Audio, Secrets, and PPRL Scope:} Exp.~4 uses locally synthesized TTS audio ($25$ calls; $20.62\%$ residual leakage under \texttt{small.en}) rather than noisy telephony speech; Exp.~3 uses short \texttt{MBPP} scripts and synthetic external credentials; and $128$-bit Bloom+Soundex PPRL is English-centric and vulnerable to frequency attacks if exported externally \citep{christen2018bloomattack}.
    \item \textbf{Portable Runtime Split:} Stage~3A WASM (secrets, anti-evasion, containers, PPRL, WAV/canvas redaction) runs live in the RTIO browser sandbox today, while Stage~3B ONNX GLiNER and local Whisper ASR currently execute in Node/Python and fail closed without local weights; OCR on dark stylized slides also benefits from modern ONNX OCR \citep{cui2025paddleocr,ibm2025granitedocling}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Versieve-MM demonstrates a verifiable zero-import WebAssembly systems architecture ($187{,}087$\,B, $0$ host imports) spanning enterprise container auditing, $2.97$\,MB/s steady-state secret scanning, client-side audio/visual redaction, and noisy cross-modal erasure. Evaluating honestly against strong third-party baselines confirms that compact $45.8$\,MB edge NER models still trail $400$\,MB server pipelines like Presidio on out-of-distribution legal text---and shows how human-calibrated, detector-informed LTT gatekeeping provides an auditable, fail-closed release layer ($0.55\%$--$4.62\%$ Conditional FPR at $6.9\%$--$12.9\%$ overall auto-release) when zero-egress constraints prohibit server-side stacks.
