Reviewer panel: 5 role-separated seats (Journal-Fit, Methodology, Domain, Perspective, Devil's Advocate), MLSys main-track bar.

## Seat 1 Journal-Fit

**S1.1 MAJOR, overlap with companion paper.** Location: Section 4.4 "Three-Model Native CAG vs. Graph-PPR and Controlled PII A/B" and Section 4.5 "Verbatim Prompt-Level Comparison", plus most of Table 1. Table 4 (tab:cag-ppr) reproduces companion Table tab:trackc nearly number-for-number (accuracy 58.3/75.0/75.0%, F1 0.557, latencies 0.42/1.00/2.10 s, the 33.3-point truncated-CAG gap, the "$\Delta = 0.0\%$" raw-vs-redacted A/B with the $-0.062$ F1 tax, the $11/12 \to 0/12$ adversarial extraction). Table 5 (tab:prompt-examples) duplicates the companion's prompt-comparison table (same oaic-01, out-01, RTIO, Elena Vance audit-note examples). The canary Safe numbers (2.70–3.32 bits, $-4.93$ to $-5.70$ bits, 0/12 Rank-1 on 1.5B) appear verbatim in the companion's abstract and Section 5.3. Why it matters: MLSys reviewers treat salami-slicing as a submission-integrity issue; roughly half of this paper's content (Sections 4.4, 4.5, the Safe side of Table 1, the entire T0+Graph-PPR vs closed-book-LoRA result in Section 4.2 item 3) is already in the companion. What is genuinely new here is exactly three things: PT-LM, the Redaction-Token Subspace Hijacking narrative, and the 18-adapter grid. Fix: rewrite the framing so this paper is titled and introduced as the PT-LM / loss-masking paper; compress Sections 4.4 and 4.5 to a cross-reference ("reported in companion, reproduced here for self-containment" with one summary table); cite the companion explicitly; state in the contribution list which results are new versus inherited.

**S1.2 MAJOR, the headline framing buries the contribution.** Location: Abstract, opening sentences. The abstract's first stated contribution is "we resolve the $90\%$--$92\%$ immediate-EOS decoding collapse seen in flat-line fine-tuning (\texttt{T1})": a debugging step from the pipeline's own history that no external reader has context for. Per the binding author style ruling (b), the title must lead with the training-data question, and "Versieve: Benchmarking Sanitized Data for..." leads with a privacy/process word and a product name instead of the actual question (can a local model be fine-tuned on redacted data without either memorizing secrets or inheriting the redaction artifacts). Fix: retitle around the training-data question (e.g., "Can Local LoRA Learn Sanitized Compliance Text? Redaction-Token Failure Modes and a Loss-Masking Fix on Consumer GPUs"), and restructure the abstract to open with the PT-LM question, not the T1 fix.

**S1.3 MINOR, scope confusion between two question statements.** Location: Section 1, the two numbered research questions. Q1 (does redaction matter) is a privacy claim; Q2 (fair-shot LoRA vs zero-training retrieval) is a utility claim; the PT-LM contribution appears nowhere in the two numbered questions and only emerges in Section 3.2. Fix: add the PT-LM question as an explicit numbered question in Section 1, since the abstract presents it as contribution two.

**S1.4 MINOR, "six open-weight models" headline is a stretch.** Location: Abstract ("across six open-weight models") and Conclusion ("Our six-model benchmark"). Three models were fine-tuned and evaluated on the main suite; the three Ollama comparators appear only in the separate 12-probe CAG section with one arm each. Fix: "three fine-tuned and three inference-only models" in both places.

Genuine strengths seen by this seat: the paired blinded-judge design with a cross-family checker is above the bar for systems papers at this venue; the RTX 4070 constraint is a real, well-chosen deployment envelope; the PT-LM result itself (tag echo 40–85% to 0/210) is a clean, reproducible-feeling effect worth an MLSys paper.

## Seat 2 Methodology

**S2.1 MAJOR, confounded comparison inside the PT-LM arm (attribution failure).** Location: Section 3.2, third bullet: PT-LM is defined as three simultaneous changes relative to T2-Chat Safe: (i) backward loss masking of placeholder subwords, (ii) forward-context substitution with grammatical role surrogates $g(\text{type})$, (iii) "scope-balancing abstention turns". The paper attributes the false-refusal drop ($20.0$–$47.5\% \to 0.0\%$) to PT-LM, but the abstention-turn rebalance is a data-level change that plausibly does most of that work, and the $10\%$ abstention turns are already in T2-Chat Safe ("$10\%$ explicit abstention turns" in the abstract describes T2-Chat). Whether Safe and PT-LM datasets are identical except for placeholders/substitution is never stated: the abstract says "matched Raw vs. Versieve-Safe pairs", but the Safe-vs-PT-LM data identity is only implicit. Why it matters: the headline "PT-LM removes in-scope false refusals" attributes a data effect to a loss effect. Concrete fix: one ablation row, Safe dataset + loss masking only (no forward substitution), and a statement of exactly which dataset rows differ between Safe and PT-LM; attribute false-refusal and tag-elimination effects separately.

**S2.2 MAJOR, the mechanism claim is not tested at the level claimed.** Location: Section 3.2, third bullet: "the rank-$r$ LoRA update gradient $\nabla_{\Delta W}\mathcal{L}_{\text{CE}}$ is disproportionately dominated by predicting $\mathcal{P}$ tokens, hijacking the low-rank adapter subspace". No gradient decomposition is reported anywhere: no per-token-class gradient-norm share, no singular-vector or cosine analysis of the adapter update, no measurement that placeholder tokens dominate the loss mass relative to their frequency. The reported evidence is an ablation (mask the tags, artifacts disappear) plus a 5.72–5.78% token share and a prior-probability argument ($p_{\theta_0} \ll 10^{-5}$). Note that aggregate gradient contribution is proportional to frequency times surprisal, and placeholder tokens are simultaneously low-prior and 5.7% of subwords: the direction of the argument is plausible, but "concentrated rank-$r$ adapter subspace" is a formal claim with no subspace measurement behind it. The simpler competing account, surface-format imitation of a frequent literal string in targets, predicts the same 40–85% echo and the same fix, and is never ruled out. See Seat 5 for the naming issue. Concrete fix: either measure the gradient share (one tensor: fraction of $\|\nabla\|$ from $\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}$ under Safe), or downgrade all "subspace hijacking" language to "placeholder-token gradient concentration / format imitation" and keep the fix contribution.

**S2.3 MAJOR, abstract and conclusion misstate the significance picture on LFM3.** Location: Abstract ("lifting seen-document token F1 by $+84\%$ to $+251\%$") and Conclusion ("lifts seen-document token F1 by $+84\%$ to $+251\%$ ($p \le 0.001$ on Qwen models)"). The LFM3 seen-F1 jump has $p = 0.083$ (honestly given in Section 4.2 item 2), non-significant. The abstract groups all three models under one "lifting" verb with no significance break; the conclusion's parenthetical scopes the p-values but still folds LFM3 into the numeric range without saying it is non-significant. Why it matters: this is the paper's own bar: the design section promises honest reporting, and the strongest cross-model claim currently overstates by including one arm that could be noise on $n=20$. Concrete fix: in the abstract and conclusion, add "(not significant on LFM3, $p = 0.083$)" to the seen-F1 range, or restrict the range to the two significant models and state LFM3 separately.

**S2.4 MAJOR, multiplicity unhandled and inferential coverage inconsistent across headline claims.** Location: Section 3.3 and Table 2. At least 12+ permutation tests are reported (3 models x {PPR-vs-Safe, PPR-vs-PT-LM, seen-F1 Safe-vs-PT-LM, plus In-F1 comparisons}), all uncorrected; the reported floor $p = 0.0005 = 1/2001$ is consistent with the 2,000-resample design, so large effects likely survive correction, but this is never addressed. Meanwhile the other headline, "nearly doubles blinded in-scope judge scores ($18.8\% \to 36.2\%$)", has no p-value and no CI anywhere, and PT-LM "surpassing both Base $0.145$ and Raw $0.129$" on 1.5B held-out F1 is asserted with a test only vs. Safe; the PT-LM CI $[0.127, 0.175]$ overlaps Base's $[0.129, 0.164]$. Concrete fix: state the test family and apply Holm or report all pairwise p-values in an appendix; add a CI or permutation p for the judge-score doubling; either test PT-LM vs Base or delete "surpassing Base".

**S2.5 MAJOR, the "at or below base" grid claim is a noise-level margin.** Location: Section 4.3 and Table 4 (tab:ptlm-sweep): "stays at or below each model's untrained base ($3.84$, $4.17$, $4.52$ bits) at every grid point; the worst case (LFM3-1.7B, 80 steps, 1e-4: $4.49$ bits) still sits below its own base." $4.49$ vs $4.52$ is 0.03 bits over a 12-canary mean with no dispersion reported; calling that "below base" presents a tie as protection. Compounding this, the grid table omits Rank-1 counts, and Table 1 shows LFM3 PT-LM at $2/12$ Rank-1 versus a base of $1/12$: a trained adapter with more fully-memorized canaries than base, while the conclusion claims only "no trained adapter exceeds its base on exposure". Concrete fix: report per-adapter Rank-1 and a dispersion estimate (bootstrap over canaries) in the grid, and replace "at or below base" with "within noise of base, never exceeding it" with the LFM3 margin stated numerically.

**S2.6 MINOR, T1-to-T2 attribution of the EOS collapse is uncontrolled.** Location: Section 3.2 T1 bullet and Section 4.1: "A boundary audit revealed double trailing EOS tokens on 46 raw and 20 safe rows"; the abstract attributes the 90–92% collapse resolution to the chat-only package. The fix changes at least four things at once (chat template, assistant-only masking, single EOS, abstention turns); nothing isolates which removed the collapse. The audit hint (double EOS) strongly suggests the single-EOS fix alone may be sufficient. Fix: one-cell control (T1 data + single clean EOS) or soften the causal language to "the chat-only package resolves the collapse".

**S2.7 MINOR, anomalies in Table 2 never discussed.** Location: Table 2, T3-Chat Safe + PPR rows. The hybrid is catastrophically worse than both of its components on 1.5B (F1 $0.092$, 90.0% false refusals) yet beats Safe alone on LFM3 ($0.242$), and its <PII> echo (4%/8%) is far below its Safe parent's (18%/8%) on held-out despite being identical adapters with retrieval glued on. These are exactly the rows a careful reader will probe; silence reads as selective attention. Fix: one paragraph explaining or flagging the hybrid's instability and what suppresses the tag echo under retrieval.

**S2.8 MINOR, reproducibility gaps.** Location: Sections 3.2, 3.3, and the whole paper. No training seeds, no judge/shuffle seeds, no decoy-generation seed ("deterministic same-format random decoys", determinism method unstated), no transformers/peft/training-framework versions (only PyTorch 2.10.0+cu128), no Python version, no wall-clock training cost, and no artifact-availability statement (no code/data release, no "receipts available" URL even though the text twice references committed receipts and a sweep artifact). The provenance of the 480 QA-turn training rows is unstated: who or what wrote them determines both dataset licensing and the validity of the seen-document probe. Fix: a reproducibility paragraph with versions, all seeds, QA-turn provenance, total train hours, and an artifact statement; a licensing line for NIST AI 100-1, OAIC guidelines, and the Privacy Act compilation.

**S2.9 MINOR, statistics precision is adequate but under-specified.** Location: Section 3.3: "1,000-resample paired bootstrap 95% CIs and 2,000-resample two-sided paired permutation p-values" does not state the pairing unit (per-question), the test statistic (mean difference of F1?), or whether judge scores were included in either. Fix: one sentence naming unit, statistic, and which metrics get which test.

Genuine strengths: the shuffled, identity-stripped dual-judge protocol; honest inline reporting of $p=0.083$ in Section 4.2; the grid's statement that the 80-step column is the grid's origin rather than a selection from it; denominators check out (40+10 held-out, 20 seen, 3 x 70 = 210 responses for the 0/210 claim; Table 2's Safe <PII> echo 85/65/40% matches the $17/20$, $13/20$, $8/20$ counts).

## Seat 3 Domain (privacy, memorization, LoRA, retrieval)

**S3.1 MAJOR, Rank-1 evidence contradicts the strongest protection wording.** Location: Table 1 and Conclusion: "cutting canary weight exposure ... down to 2.63–3.51 bits under Safe and PT-LM; 0/12 Rank-1 on Qwen2.5-1.5B". The 0/12 claim is the single best cell of nine Safe/PT-LM model cells; the other cells retain 1–2 Rank-1 memorized canaries (0.5B Safe 1/12, 0.5B PT-LM 1/12, LFM3 Safe 1/12, LFM3 PT-LM 2/12, and LFM3 PT-LM above its own base's 1/12). Redaction reduces but does not fully suppress weight-level memorization, and the abstract's "reduces ... down to" phrasing plus the single highlighted 0/12 cell invites the reading that rank-1 memorization is eliminated. Fix: report all nine Rank-1 cells in the abstract or conclusion, or scope the 0/12 claim to its model explicitly every time it appears.

**S3.2 MAJOR, no lexical retrieval baseline.** Location: Sections 4.2 and 4.4, and Table 4. Graph-PPR is never compared against BM25 or any dense retriever; the Ollama 12-probe table has Base, Graph-PPR, and CAG arms only. The paper's retrieval contribution ("sub-millisecond WebAssembly Graph-PPR") is therefore unbenchmarked against the standard cheap alternative, and the "beats LoRA" gap does not isolate what the graph adds over BM25. Fix: add a BM25 arm to the 50-question suite (it is zero-GPU and cheap) or explicitly scope the claim to "retrieval-augmented vs. closed-book" without implying the graph structure is why.

**S3.3 MINOR, related-work coverage is thin on exactly the relevant literatures.** Location: Section 2. Missing: machine unlearning and its GDPR connection (the companion markets Art. 17 erasure; this paper inherits the framing), PII-anonymization tooling (e.g., Presidio-style NER masking as the standard alternative to regex), extraction-based memorization measurement (Carlini et al. extraction metrics beyond the exposure metric), LoRA capacity work beyond the single cited adapter-memorization reference. Also missing: any justification of the salted-pseudonym vault's re-identification threat model (who holds the vault, and does Art. 17 erasure delete it). Fix: one related-work paragraph per missing area, and a threat-model sentence for the vault.

**S3.4 MINOR, canary protocol inherits the exposure metric's known limitations without comment.** Location: Section 3.3: summed next-token log-probability rank over 1,000 decoys, 12 canaries of which only 4 are at 20x repetition; the headline 8.25–8.40-bit numbers rest on those 4 canaries. Rank-based exposure under ties ("$r$ is the canary's mean rank under ties") is stated, which is good, but no CIs over canaries are given and canary entropy/format is not described (single-token vs multi-token secrets changes exposure behavior). Fix: state canary token length and composition, and bootstrap the 12-canary mean.

**S3.5 MINOR, the 18-adapter grid arithmetic checks out but its framing overstates what it rules out.** Location: Section 4.3: "Longer training does not buy memorization because the secrets are absent from the training bytes, not merely down-weighted." The grid varies PT-LM only; a Raw-arm grid (secrets in bytes, 3x budget) would be needed to assert that longer training under redaction is not merely insufficient, and the Raw cells at 80 steps already show 8+ bits, so the direction is plausible but the "because" sentence is an unsupported causal gloss. Also verified positive: the $2.62$–$4.49$-bit span, the 0.0% abstention on all six 80-step adapters, and the $4.49 < 4.52$ worst-case ordering all match the table. Fix: soften to "consistent with absence of the secrets from the training bytes" or run one Raw long-budget cell.

**S3.6 MINOR, T2-Chat Safe seen-F1 regression vs. base is a finding, not a footnote.** Location: Table 2: on 0.5B, Safe seen F1 $0.077$ is well below the untrained base's $0.193$, and on 1.5B Safe $0.103$ vs base $0.202$. Training on redacted soup actively degrades recall below doing nothing: this is the strongest evidence in the paper for the hijacking story and it is never singled out. Fix: one sentence in Section 4.2 making this the evidence anchor for the mechanism section.

## Seat 4 Perspective (deployment, stakeholder costs, broader implications)

**S4.1 MAJOR, the recommended recipe has no functioning refusal boundary, and the paper does not warn the operator.** Location: Section 4.3 and the abstract's framing. The headline recipe is 80 optimizer steps; the grid shows every 80-step adapter abstains on 0.0% of out-of-scope questions, while abstention only appears at 160–240 steps. A compliance operator adopting the paper's main configuration gets a model that never refuses out-of-scope questions, which is a liability posture in a compliance context. The conclusion's "out-of-scope abstention ... emerges beyond 80 optimizer steps" is buried relative to the recipe the paper trains and recommends. Fix: an explicit deployment guidance sentence at the grid section and the conclusion: at the default 80-step recipe, refusal behavior must come from the retrieval layer or a classifier, not the adapter.

**S4.2 MAJOR, "zero-accuracy-loss security boundary" overstates what was measured for the people downstream.** Location: Conclusion and companion-inherited framing. Accuracy is preserved ($\Delta = 0.0\%$ on a 9/12 suite) but the same section reports a $-0.062$ F1 tax and Table 5 shows the concrete failure mode for the reader of redacted text: "maintain a clearly expressed and up-to-date APP \texttt{<PII:PERSON\_04>}". Whoever consumes Versieve output (a human reading a redacted brief, or a downstream model) receives corrupted legal phrases. Two different stakeholders, two different costs: the operator sees accuracy, the human reader sees broken text. Fix: replace "zero-accuracy-loss" with "accuracy-preserving at a measured lexical cost" in the conclusion, and keep the Table 5 example referenced there.

**S4.3 MINOR, vault-side security is the deployment-critical unknown and is absent.** Location: Section 3 and Abstract: salted pseudonymization plus a vault is reversible by design. The paper never states where the vault lives, what its compromise means (full re-identification of every redacted document), or what the Art. 17 story is for the vault (the companion markets 1 ms KV eviction, which is not document erasure). For the target reader (someone choosing between redaction pipelines for real records), this is the question they must answer first. Fix: a threat-model paragraph: vault custody, key management, and the explicit statement that pseudonymization is not anonymization.

**S4.4 MINOR, the data subjects and document provenance are invisible.** Location: Section 3: the corpus is real regulation text (NIST, OAIC, Privacy Act) with synthetic PII; that is safe, and to the paper's credit no real-person claim appears. But the 480 synthetic QA turns' generation process is unstated (see S2.8), so a reader cannot tell if any model-generated text enters the training set, which matters for both licensing and for the honesty of "fully local". Fix: covered by the S2.8 provenance fix; state it from the data-subject/deployment angle in one sentence.

## Seat 5 Devil's Advocate

**S5.1 CRITICAL (the paper's core tension), the paper refutes the significance of its own headline fix.** Location: Abstract and Conclusion, contributions 2 and 3 in sequence. The paper's second contribution is that PT-LM rescues redacted-document LoRA (tag echo 40–85% to 0.0%, seen F1 up to $+251\%$, judge scores nearly doubling). The third contribution is that, even after this rescue, zero-training retrieval still beats every LoRA arm on every model ($p = 0.0005$) at lower latency, and the recommended deployment ("for compliance QA on consumer GPUs ... delivers the strongest accuracy-latency Pareto frontier") is the retrieval arm. Read as guidance, the paper says: do not use the pipeline whose failure mode the paper fixes. The only honest defense of PT-LM's significance is the case where fine-tuning on redacted text is required for reasons outside this paper (latency-insensitive weight-held knowledge, non-QA generation). That case is never made in one sentence anywhere. Fix: state the deployment niche for PT-LM explicitly (e.g., when the corpus is too volatile for re-indexing or when generation must be weight-held), or accept that the paper's practical conclusion is "use retrieval" and restructure the abstract so PT-LM is presented as a documented failure mode of the redaction-as-data recipe, not as the headline contribution.

**S5.2 MAJOR, "Redaction-Token Subspace Hijacking" is a post-hoc mechanistic narrative over an ablation.** Location: Section 3.2, the bolded name. Three checks fail. (i) No subspace measurement exists (S2.2). (ii) The competing surface-format-imitation account predicts the identical outcomes and is not ruled out. (iii) The name itself commits to low-rank-subspace dynamics that were never measured; "Privacy-Token Loss Masking" as a fix name is fine, but "hijacking" and "subspace" are borrowed credibility. Also the mechanism section's prior-probability argument ($p_{\theta_0} \ll 10^{-5}$) is asserted without any measured surprisal. Fix: rename to something like "placeholder-token gradient capture" and keep "subspace" only if a measurement is added; the mechanism claim as written would not survive an MLSys reviewer asking "show me the gradient attribution".

**S5.3 MAJOR, every summary cell that flatters is checked and one pattern recurs: best-of-matrix highlighting.** Locations inventory: (i) abstract/conclusion "$0/12$ Rank-1 on Qwen2.5-1.5B" while LFM3 PT-LM sits at 2/12 above its own base (S3.1). (ii) Abstract "nearly doubling ... judge scores (18.8% to 36.2% on Qwen2.5-1.5B)": the 1.5B cell is the most favorable of the three (2.6x on 0.5B, 1.4x on LFM3 are not "nearly double" and are not mentioned as such, though they are reported elsewhere). (iii) Abstract's false-refusal range "30%–47.5%" omits LFM3's 20% safe-baseline value that the conclusion correctly includes ("20%–47.5%"). (iv) The seen-F1 "+84% to +251%" range with no LFM3 significance marker (S2.3). None of these are fabrications; table 2 is complete and honest. The pattern is in the prose summaries, which is where readers will quote it. Fix: sweep the abstract and conclusion so every range and highlighted cell matches the appendix-level honesty of the tables.

**S5.4 MAJOR, the "fair-shot" framing is loaded in the LoRA's disfavor and the load is not disclosed in the comparisons it drives.** Location: Abstract ("Can fair-shot local LoRA beat zero-training retrieval?"), Section 3.2: 80 steps on 636 rows for a corpus of 22,539 unique chunks, so the closed-book arms see roughly 2% of the corpus' content in a form optimized for chat turn-taking, while the PPR arm sees all of it at inference. That is a legitimate deployment comparison (retrieval genuinely is the deployment), but calling the LoRA arm "fair-shot" while its information budget is two orders of magnitude smaller is tendrous, and the 18-adapter grid rescues the canary claim but is run only in the PT-LM arm, so the utility-side "80 steps is the budget" objection is answered only indirectly (best grid judge 45.0%, still below PPR's 52.5% on the strict judge, which is the one grid cell that does partially rebuts under-training; this should be said rather than left implicit). Fix: rename or define "fair-shot" precisely (matched compute, not matched data), and add one sentence using the grid's 45.0% best cell against the under-training objection.

**S5.5 MINOR, the "so what" for the retrieval-vs-LoRA result is thin.** The finding that retrieval beats parametric memorization for long-tail compliance facts is already established (cited: ovadia2023finetuning, mallen2023when, kandpal2023longtail) on larger models; what this paper adds is the confirmation at 0.5–1.7B on one 8 GB GPU with a privacy twist. That is a legitimate systems-benchmark niche, but the contribution paragraph should position it as replication-amplification with a new hardware envelope, not as a new discovery. Fix: one sentence in Section 2 making the inherited-vs-added split explicit.

**S5.6 MINOR, the T1 narrative is the paper arguing with its own past drafts.** Location: Abstract sentence 2, Section 4.1, Section 4.3. Ruling (d): "The paper must not argue with its own past drafts." The abstract opens its contribution list by resolving a collapse "seen in flat-line fine-tuning (T1)", T1's numbers appear in Section 4.1 and Figure/Table 5 rows, and Section 4.3 defends the single-point result against "the obvious objection": three separate places where the paper litigates its own pipeline history rather than the current design. Ruling (c) also fails here: the DeepSeek-judge deviation remark in Section 4.3 is unpublished-work meta-commentary ("the planned DeepSeek agent-swarm judge could not run on this machine ... the swarm votes backfill on a re-run") and must be cut; the "obvious objection is under-training ... not merely down-weighted" construction is the banned negation-contrast frame ("learned, not default" is a second instance). Fix: cut T1 to one design-motivation paragraph with its numbers in a footnote; delete the DeepSeek sentence; rewrite "not merely down-weighted" and "learned, not default" as plain statements. Ruling (a): the manuscript uses "---" as a rhetorical separator at least 7 times in prose (abstract x2, Section 2, Section 3.3, Section 4.2 item 2, Conclusion x2); the 13 "(---)" table cells are a defensible N/A marker but should be "--" or "n/a" to be safe. Ruling (b): "Versieve: Benchmarking Sanitized Data for..." leads with the privacy/process word (S1.2).

**Positive results the adversarial pass confirms rather than breaks:** the "secrets absent from training bytes" design (canaries excluded from Safe arms) is the correct causal structure and survives the grid; the denominator chains (3 x 70 = 210, 17+13+8/20 pattern) all reproduce; the Table 2 numbers are internally consistent with every prose claim checked; Table 4's "at or below base" claim holds on mean exposure for all 18 adapters; the 4.49 vs 4.52 worst case is honestly reported rather than hidden.

## Editorial Decision (Major Revision) + rationale

**Major Revision.** The PT-LM contribution is real, the canary pipeline is methodologically sound, and the honesty inside the tables is above average for a systems paper. But the paper in its current form (i) duplicates roughly half of its content from the companion's Section 5.3, (ii) makes a bolded mechanistic claim ("subspace hijacking") supported only by an ablation and an unmeasured gradient argument, (iii) attributes a compound intervention's effects to its loss-level component without an ablation, (iv) lets its abstract and conclusion overstate significance on LFM3 and cherry-pick the best Rank-1 cell, and (v) violates four of the four binding author style rulings in load-bearing places (title, em-dashes, meta-commentary, arguing with T1). All of these are fixable without new training runs except the two-component ablation (one Safe-data + masking-only run per model, or a defensible argument why the components cannot be disentangled). None of them is a fatal flaw in the experimental work itself.

## Consensus and disagreements

- All five seats converge on: overlap with the companion must be resolved (S1.1), the mechanism language must be measured or renamed (S2.2/S5.2), and the abstract/conclusion significance sweep is needed (S2.3/S5.3).
- Disagreement, resolved toward MAJOR: Seat 2 rated the T3-Chat hybrid anomalies (S2.7) MINOR because they do not load a headline claim; Seat 5 argued MAJOR because the hybrid is the only equal-information-access arm and its collapse undermines the utility comparison. Resolved MINOR with a required explanation because the paper's claims rest on the T0-vs-LoRA gap, not the hybrid.
- Disagreement, resolved toward CRITICAL: Seat 5's core-tension finding (S5.1) was rated CRITICAL while other seats saw it as a framing problem. Resolved: it is listed as THE critical adjudication below, but the decision is Major Revision rather than Reject because the tension has a defensible resolution (the PT-LM deployment niche).
- No seat disputed the canary pipeline's design, the blinded-judge protocol, or any arithmetic that was checked; no seat found a fabricated, contradictory, or unsupported number inside a table.

## DA CRITICAL adjudication

R1 (the paper's core claim tension) is confirmed as the single critical issue. The sequence "PT-LM rescues redaction-trained LoRA" followed, two sentences later, by "zero-training retrieval still beats all closed-book LoRA including PT-LM across every model at lower latency, and we recommend retrieval" leaves PT-LM without an articulated user. This does not invalidate the result: the failure mode is real, the fix is clean, and the grid shows PT-LM's protection is recipe-robust with plausible utility headroom (LFM3 at 160/1e-4 reaches 45.0% strict-judge). But the manuscript must name the deployment niche before the claim can carry a headline. If no niche is nameable, this is two papers (a failure-mode note and a retrieval benchmark) rather than one, and the paper should be restructured accordingly, which would also dissolve R2.

## Not verified / limits of this review

- All numeric claims are cross-checked only against the manuscript's own tables, the companion paper's Section 5.3, and internal arithmetic. No underlying receipts, sweep artifacts, per-response logs, or training code were opened.
- Figure panels were not rendered or inspected; caption claims (Panel A/B content) are unverified.
- External literature existence checks were not performed; `tan_lora_memorization` (arXiv:2607.21351) and the 2026 model-card entries are taken on faith. All cited works are also formatted as @misc with arXiv-in-howpublished even where venues exist (Hu et al. ICLR 2022, Carlini et al. IEEE S&P 2019, Lukas et al. - Whether the 1,000/2,000-resample tests were actually performed as described, and whether the reported p-value floor (0.0005 = 1/2001) matches the code, could not be verified without artifacts.

- Llama-judge columns in Table 2 (All, 70 questions) vs. the grid's llama column (held-out in-scope, 40 questions) are not directly comparable; the review could not confirm the two llama protocols are as described.
