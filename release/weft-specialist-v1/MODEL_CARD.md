# Weft specialist model card: weft-specialist-v1

Date: 2026-11-28. Tickets: #47 (W7, this card), #45 (W5, the shipped artifacts),
#46 (W6, the theme-node seam). The shipped release artifacts are the W5
quantized ONNX exports of the Weft specialist: an int4 MatMulNBits artifact
and an int8 dynamic reference, both cut from the same W4-v4 trained state.

## 1. What this is

The Weft specialist is a small pairwise scorer for cross-document theme
linking that requires no person signal. Architecture:

- Base: all-MiniLM-L6-v2 (6 layers, 384 dims), frozen throughout training
  (learning rate 0.0 on the base).
- Heads: int8 link, theme, and evidence heads trained on top of the frozen
  base.
- Shipped artifact (int4): one ONNX file containing the tokenized pair graph,
  the int8 per-row word-embedding table (30522 rows, Gather plus Cast plus
  scale Mul dequantization), the int4 MatMulNBits FFN and attention-output
  matmuls, the int8 heads, and the metadata props carrying the tokenizer
  JSON, the int8 k-means OOD bank (k=1024), the OOD threshold, and the
  manifest of sha-256 pins (W4-v4 model.pt, tokenizer, v4 train and eval
  jsonl). Tokenization happens outside the graph; everything else loads from
  the single file.
- int8 dynamic reference: the same exported pair graph from the fp32 state,
  quantized int8 dynamic, no metadata manifest inside.
- Training corpus: the v4 fullscale live pair corpus (48000 train / 2000 eval
  pairs, 6 voices), synthetic multi-format estates generated by this
  repository.

The release weights are not shipped in this repository (trained weights stay
on disk under a gitignored path; the digests are pinned in
`RELEASE-MANIFEST.json` and `results/weft-export-latency.json`). The
reproduction recipe regenerates them end to end; see the manifest.

## 2. Intended use

Use the specialist to score document pairs for likely shared theme when
identity should not matter: cross-department and cross-modality linking on
enterprise-style documents in English, inside the 240-word envelope of the
v4 corpus family, with the conformal abstain rule deciding whether an answer
is answered or routed. The artifact expects the W5 loading path (fail-closed
typed errors when weights, metadata, or the runtime are absent; there is no
fallback similarity score anywhere in the path).

Do not use it for:

- unseen voices or unseen styles without human review routing; the transfer
  cell below measures 0.6695 and the W4/W5 verdicts route that gap to
  review rather than to a silent merge;
- long documents; nothing here reaches past the 240-word envelope;
- any claim that identity is removed; the model is identity-blind in the
  measured-probe sense only (section 5);
- a privacy mechanism by itself; the privacy seams (vault, salting,
  gatekeeper, erasure) live elsewhere in the product, and this scorer makes
  no erasure claim.

### Release gate (ruled by the founder, passed)

The story-36 acceptance criterion (the size-matched static baseline must be
beaten by the ruled scoring path) was recorded as blocked pending the
founder's scoring-path ruling, because on the synthetic estate's
identity_disjoint bucket the static embedding baseline (model2vec
POTION-32M) scores F1 0.9973, the specialist's link-head path scores 0.3617,
and the pooled cosine path scores 0.9989. The founder ruled on 2026-11-28:
Weft's purpose was never identity-based linking; the product point is theme
linking, so the ruled scoring path is the specialist's pooled-cosine
theme-linking score, which beats the size-matched static baseline (0.9989
versus 0.9973). The gate is recorded `ruled-passed` in the manifest's
`releaseGate` block with the ruling text and the prior blocked status
preserved. The link head is rescoped to the abstention and calibration layer
over the theme-linking score; its 0.3617 on this bucket is retained as the
honest measurement of the head's own behavior, unchanged by the ruling.
One measurement caveat travels with the ruling: BM25 scores 1.0 on this
bucket because same-theme documents in the synthetic estate share
vocabulary, so the bucket measures lexical overlap as well as theme
adjacency; a paraphrase-hard benchmark stratum is filed as the follow-up
that measures learned theme linking specifically.

## 4. Failure modes and limits

Stated honestly, each one measured:

1. **Unseen-voice transfer is partial.** The 6-voice v4 head transfers to
   the unseen voice at F1 0.6695 (precision 0.5737). Covered-style
   competence is not style-robust competence.
2. **Single-voice collapse.** A single-voice-trained specialist collapses to
   F1 0.4798 under style variance. The head is only as wide as its training
   styles.
3. **The OOD tripwire is style-blind.** The kNN bank catches corpus-level
   shift; on the unseen-voice live eval (the v2 corpus) it flags only
   6.7 percent of documents (the W5 note, section 4; the ledger's 0.0669
   entry is the v4-eval tripwire rate), so the flag rate carries no signal
   about the style-voice gap. The out-of-coverage route for unseen style
   cannot rely on the tripwire alone; low-margin routing hooks are the
   seam.
4. **Conformal coverage is thin.** The certified rule answers 16.1 percent
   of pairs; the head's scores are sharply bimodal at the 0.5 boundary, so
   coverage cannot grow without admitting a dense block of negatives and
   breaking the bound. The abstain rule certifies and fails closed; the
   rest of the traffic routes to review. The rule itself is prior art
   (Learn-Then-Test over a threshold sequence); no new calibration method
   is claimed.
5. **The 240-word envelope stands.** The corpus tops out at 240 words; no
   long-document competence claim is made.
6. **Theme thresholds are uncalibrated on heterogeneous corpora.** On the
   synthetic estate the agglomeration emits 1 theme node (30 members) from
   383 scored pairs (287 joins, 96 abstains, 97 identity-disjoint joins, 69
   disagreements recorded). That is the construction-valid answer for a
   topically homogeneous corpus, not a quality claim; threshold
   recalibration needs a corpus with more than one theme to mean anything.
7. **Identity-blindness is a measured probe result, not a guarantee** (the
   caveat below is quoted verbatim; section 5).

## 5. Identity-blindness (worded per the ruling)

The ruling fixes the wording: identity-blind, based on a measured post-hoc
probe framing, with the probe family fixed and reported. The model makes no
removal claim and no erasure claim. The reason the wording is a ruling comes
from the primary literature: adversarially trained removal leaves post-hoc
recovery open (Elazar and Goldberg, section 6 of this card), and the probe
family matters: the INLP text concedes nonlinear recovery in the same setup
where an RBF-SVM stays near chance, so the card fixes its probe family.

Verbatim caution quoted in the record: "do not rely on the adversarial
training to achieve invariant representation to sensitive features", and
the training adversary score "cannot be trusted" and must be verified "with
an externally-trained attacker, preferably on unseen data" (Elazar and
Goldberg, EMNLP 2018).

Masked-eval evidence (v4 masked eval, 40 identity-masked positives of 2000):
masking identity-bearing tokens before scoring does not hurt at the usable
levels (4-bit masked F1 0.9635 against 0.9572 unmasked). That reading is
measured on the v4 masked eval only, with the 240-word caveat carried.

Dated update, 2026-11-28 (the post-hoc probe, now measured; issue #60, PR
#62): an externally trained 1-hidden-layer ReLU MLP recovers the primary
identity key above chance from every condition's pooled output on the
committed W1 synthetic estate (240 documents, 16 identity keys, stratified
180 train / 60 eval, seed 20261127, chance baseline 0.100): fp32 MiniLM
control 0.4167, shipped int4 0.4500, shipped int8 0.3500. An RBF-SVM on the
same representations stays at or below chance (0.1000, 0.1167, and 0.0500,
the last recorded with belowChance set), replicating the probe-family
specific reading from the INLP record (section 6): nonlinear recovery
depends on the probe family, which is why this card fixes and reports its
probe family. The verdict, reported plainly: identity is recoverable above
chance in the MLP probe family, quantization does not erase the signal, and
identity-blind remains exactly what this section always claimed: a scoped
measured-probe framing with no removal claim and no guarantee. Traces:
results/weft-h2-identity-probe.json and
docs/research-notes/2026-11-28-h2-identity-probe.md.

## 7. Novelty statement

The surviving conjunction, in exactly the audited wording: the Weft
combination (identity-blind pairwise theme linking over document-graph
structure, an abstain-or-route calibrated path around a specialist head, and
the fail-closed release packaging for it) is not found in prior art as of
this audit. No single component is claimed as new; the components are cited
and differentiated row by row above, and every one is prior art on its own.
The audit's recorded limits apply (the record, section 4): OpenAlex indexing
of 2026 preprints is incomplete, INLP's reference list went unread, and the
"unoccupied" reading is only as strong as the citation graph.

Dated correction, 2026-11-28 (the framing pass under the third ruling):
the pinned Weft phrase above, "identity-blind pairwise theme linking over
document-graph structure", should read "source-orthogonal
(person-independent) pairwise theme linking over document-graph structure":
knowledge is the unit, source and theme are the axes, and identity is
reserved for the person erasure axis only. The original pinned text stays
in place exactly as audited; the corrected wording is what the papers print
from this pass forward. Reference:
docs/research-notes/2026-11-28-company-brain-premise-correction.md.
