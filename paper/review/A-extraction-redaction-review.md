# Review: Versieve: Benchmarking Deterministic Document Extraction, Data Redaction, and Conformal Gatekeeping

Panel: academic-paper-reviewer (academic-research-skills v1.11.1), five role-separated seats.
Target venue: MLSys main track. Field: systems for ML, privacy-preserving document curation, conformal risk control.

**Note on role separation:** the five seats below are analytic positions taken by a single reviewer panel, not five independent reviewers. They share one evidence base and one reviewer; they are designed to force every finding through five distinct lenses, not to simulate five independent error processes. Cross-seat agreement therefore carries somewhat less evidentiary weight than agreement between five separate humans, and we say so plainly.

## Seat 1 Journal-Fit

**Overall.** The paper's unique core (Sections 2, 3, 4: deterministic ingestion, FUNSD tiling geometry, regex recall ceiling, fail-closed LTT gatekeeping, substrate comparison) is a legitimate MLSys contribution. Section 5.3 is not; it is a compressed duplicate of the companion paper's results.

- **[CRITICAL] Section 5.3 is a companion-paper duplicate, not a shared experiment.** Location: Section 5.3, "Cross-Family Local LoRA vs.\ Zero-Training Graph-PPR". Evidence: a 6-gram overlap measure between the two `.tex` files finds 255 shared 6-grams of 4,432 (5.8% of tokens), and the shared grams are not incidental boilerplate: they are the OAIC/Graph-PPR table values (`58.3%`, `0.438`, `0.42 s`, `75.0%`, `0.557`, `2.10 s`, `11.8x`--`16.6x`, `0.62 ms`, `33.3` accuracy points, `11/12 -> 0/12`, `8.25`--`8.40` bits), the `$-0.062$ F1 tax`, the `636`-row / `89`-doc / `104`-canary dataset description, and the verbatim prompt-comparison table (the identical "central objective of APP~1" exchange, `src/...` companion lines 256-344). The companion additionally contains the PT-LM method contribution, dual cross-family judges, and an 18-adapter robustness grid. Why it matters: the companion is strictly stronger on the material this paper duplicates; two MLSys submissions reporting the same tables, prompts, and judge from one codebase is textbook salami slicing, and an MLSys reviewer who sees both will treat this paper's Track C as prior work or fraud-adjacent self-duplication. Minimum fix: cut Section 5.3 to one short paragraph plus a citation to the companion paper, keeping only the one-sentence results needed to justify the gate/curation pipeline's downstream value.
- **[MAJOR] No related-work section and no external baseline system.** Location: after Section 1 (absent) and Section 4 as a whole. The paper cites `presidio2021` in the intro ("cloud de-identification services \citep{ai4privacy2023,presidio2021}") but never positions itself against any published extraction/redaction pipeline, and has zero comparisons except internal ones (`naive regex scrubber`, `Raw unfiltered`). Why it matters: at MLSys main track, a systems paper that never says where it sits relative to even one alternative architecture reads as an internal tech report. Minimum fix: add a short related-work section covering Wasm edge-data systems, de-identification pipelines, conformal risk control deployments, and cite at least one non-internal baseline you run.
- **[MAJOR] Binding style ruling (b) violated: the title leads with mechanism/privacy words and never touches the training-data question.** Location: `\mlsystitle{Versieve: Benchmarking Deterministic Document Extraction, Data Redaction, and Conformal Gatekeeping}`. All three title nouns are mechanism words; the training-data question (which is the founder's binding framing for this suite) is absent, and keywords likewise lead with "WebAssembly, conformal prediction". Minimum fix: retitle so the training-data/curation question leads; move mechanism terms to subtitle or keywords.
- **[MINOR] Scale of evidence may be read as too thin for a full paper.** Location: Table 1 ("PMC full text ... $8$", "$12$-probe ... benchmark"). Eight PDFs, 500 ai4privacy records, 12 OAIC probes, 50-question suite. This is defensible only if Sections 2-4 (the unique core) carry the paper; after cutting 5.3 the unique core should be extended, not padded with duplicated downstream results.
- **Genuine strengths acknowledged here:** the ingestion/tiling/LTT core is distinct from all three companions and is real work; the fail-closed honesty (reporting a certification failure instead of hiding it) is unusual and reviewer-positive.

## Seat 2 Methodology

- **[CRITICAL] The suite is not reconstructible from the paper.** Location: Section 3, "unified $1{,}207$-document public corpus". The paper never states which 500 `ai4privacy-200k` records, which 500 PubMedQA abstracts, which 8 PMC PDFs (the bib entry `miu_2017_pmc5773420` is a single 2017 article standing in for 8 documents), or which 12 OAIC probes; no seed is given for the stratified split; no code or artifact link exists anywhere in the manuscript. Why it matters: every headline number (recall percentages, LTT sweep, BCE, R@5) is a function of the specific slice, and none of it can be re-derived. Minimum fix: ship a manifest (dataset row IDs / PMC DOIs / probe set), state split seeds, add a code+artifact availability statement, list the 8 PMCs individually.
- **[CRITICAL] Label circularity in the conformal guarantee (see also Seat 5).** Location: Section 3, "Under the format-validated scanner, $826$ documents are labeled unsafe". The safe/unsafe calibration labels are produced by the same format-validated regex scanner the gatekeeper supervises. The LTT guarantee therefore certifies agreement with a scanner whose own recall on true entity types is $31.0\%$ ($180/580$, Section 4). A chunk containing regex-missed PII (bare names, addresses, employers) can be certified `KEEP\_SAFE`. Why it matters: the paper's central system promise chain (gate certifies -> export -> "certified exports", Figure 1 caption) breaks at exactly the scan- miss point, and the guarantee's wording never tells the reader this. Minimum fix: either re-derive labels from ai4privacy gold spans for the calibration experiment, or explicitly scope every "certified"/"safe" phrasing as "certified against the format-validated labeling rule" and add one test measuring true-PII leakage through the full gate+export path on regex-missed entity types.
- **[MAJOR] Statistical reporting is incomplete.** Location: Section 5.3, "all paired permutation $p = 0.0005$ on F1 and $p = 0.001$ on blinded judge". No resample count, no CIs (the companion states 1,000-resample bootstrap and 2,000-resample permutation), no multiplicity note across the three model comparisons. Why it matters: $p=0.001$ with a 2,000-resample permutation is the resolution floor, not evidence strength; and point estimates like `$55.0\%$ vs $23.0\%$` on n=50 need intervals for a main-track claims-vs-evidence fit. Minimum fix: state resample counts, report the CIs (they already exist in the companion receipts), note the trivially-holding Bonferroni correction.
- **[MAJOR] Two methods are presented with zero evaluation.** Location: Section 5.5 paragraph "Hierarchical Cluster-Robust Conformal Risk Control (\texttt{HC-CRC}) and Streaming Martingales (\texttt{E-LTT})": `$\texttt{calibrateClusterRobustGatekeeper}$ and $\texttt{calibrateStreamingEProcessGatekeeper}$ in $\texttt{src/gatekeeper.js}$`. Formula-only, numbers are absent as none of the corpus tables exercise either method; the DEFF derivation is asserted, and intra-cluster correlation `\rho > 0` is never measured on this corpus. Why it matters: unevaluated equations are not results and will not survive review at any venue. Minimum fix: either run HC-CRC/E-LTT on the LTT corpus (clusters per document are naturally available) and add numbers, or cut the paragraph to one sentence with a pointer to a future work / separate paper.
- **[MAJOR] Abstract/conclusion model-count contradiction.** Location: abstract, "a six-model open-weight compliance evaluation" vs conclusion, "five local open-weight LLMs". Counting the actual evals: 3 Ollama inference models (`lfm3:latest`, `llama3.2:3b`, `qwen2.5:7b`) + 3 LoRA models (`Qwen2.5-0.5B`, `Qwen2.5-1.5B`, `LFM3-1.7B-Base`) = 6 configurations, 5 distinct weights. Why it matters: both abstracts and conclusions are read in isolation; they currently disagree with each other about the paper's own experimental footprint. Minimum fix: pick one count and one sentence reconciling it ("six model configurations spanning five distinct weights", with the LFM3 overlap stated). Also: the "six-model" claim's downstream evaluation only actually appears in the companion, per Seat 1's CRITICAL finding.
- **[MINOR] Table 5 cell "Non-exempt" is an undefined quantity alongside numeric `$0\%$`, `$1.05\%$`, `$100\%$` values in the same PII Exposure column.** Location: Table 5, "Naive regex scrubber ... Non-exempt". Define the column's units and what makes a value "exempt", and explain the non-monotonic 0%/0%/1.05% pattern in the table (not only in the prose).
- **[MINOR] "Unfiltered" gate accuracy denominators are ambiguous.** Location: Section 5.2, "and $31.4\%$ unfiltered". 31.4% is exactly the safe rate (94/299), which implies accuracy is measured only on safe-correct predictions, not all-correct classification or anything with an unsafe-all-true baseline of 68.56%. Define what "Gate Acc" measures against which baseline.
- **[MINOR] Multi-axis conjunction arithmetic needs its precedence rule.** Location: Table 4 row "Conjunction ... $0$ kept / $85$ abstain / $215$ drop", against per-axis abstain rates of 7.3% / 79.7% / 40.0%. The combined abstain rate (28.3%) is well below the `useful` axis's own abstain rate (79.7%), which is only consistent if a drop from any axis preempts an abstention from another; that precedence rule is never stated. Minimum fix: one sentence stating the aggregation rule (`drop` > `abstain` > `keep`) and confirming per-axis numbers sum.
- **[MINOR] Arithmetic verified clean elsewhere:** splits sum (607+301+299=1,207; 415+206+205=826; 192+95+94=381); BinomCDF values correct (`$0.99^{206}=0.1261$`, `$0.99^{301}=0.0486$`, ceiling `$\lceil\ln 0.05/\ln 0.99\rceil = 299$`); Table 3 sweep is monotone and its certificate labels follow the stated rule; startup ratios (`121.6×`, `7.7x`), FUNSD survival rates, lexicon reduction factors, `53.7%` HTML strip, `-0.062` F1 tax all recompute correctly; `$8.25-3.32 = 4.93$` and `$8.40-2.70 = 5.70$` confirm the abstract's `$4.93$--$5.70$ bits` range. Credit is due for that level of internal consistency.

## Seat 3 Domain

- **[MAJOR] No external NER/de-identification baseline despite a domain claim that depends on one.** Location: Section 5.1 conclusion ("Thus, regex excels on rigidly formatted identifiers"), Section 7 bullet "Adding a compact Apache-2.0 local neural span tagger is a clear next step." For claims of the form "deterministic regex has an inherent recall ceiling on unstructured spans", the reader needs one zero-shot neural baseline (Microsoft Presidio, or GLiNER/Apache-2.0 NER) run on the same 500 ai4privacy records at the same hardware. Without it, the ceiling could be an implementation or prompt-engineering artifact, and the field-standard interpretation of "recall ceiling" (comparable to published NER numbers) is unavailable. Minimum fix: add a Presidio and/or GLiNER column beside the regex row; either the comparison survives (great) or the ceiling claim is retracted/scaled to "regex-only".
- **[MAJOR] Methodological borrowing without citation.** Location: Section 5.4, "12 Carlini Secret-Sharer canaries (max $9.97$ bits)" and Section 5.3, "$104$ Carlini canary rows". Carlini et al.'s Secret Sharer and Extracting Training Data methodology is used by name twice with no `\citep` (the companion paper does cite `carlini_secret_sharer`; this manuscript does not). Related: MCP, Ollama, Qwen2.5, Llama 3.2, LFM3, OAIC APP guidelines, PageRank, Louvain, Tarjan SCC, DuckDB are all named with zero citations. Minimum fix: `\citep{carlini_secret_sharer}` at first mention, plus a citation pass over Section 3 and 5's named third-party systems and algorithms (the intro's citation budget is currently 11 keys, 4 of which it never calls on: `haas2017wasm`, `jangda2019notsofast`, `bates2021distributionfree`, `vovk2005algorithmic`; the WebAssembly formal paper is a natural cite for a WebAssembly-first systems paper).
- **[MAJOR] Threat model: "verified" and "asserted" are not distinguished.** Location: Section 1 item 3, "audited to have zero host imports" and Appendix A, "pass automated SHA-256 provenance and zero-import verification (\texttt{npm run audit-wasm}: $\texttt{allVerified = true}$)". The zero-import and hash provenance checks are self-run audit scripts (self-verified); "zero-egress" is asserted for the MCP server but not demonstrated (e.g., a strace/packet capture); "deterministic" is demonstrated for specific kernels (same-score JS/C99) but no end-to-end replay/audit of a full redaction run is shown; and the salted-pseudonym vault's own security is unaddressed (salt provenance, vault compromise, lookup-table reversal of low-entropy PII like names). Why it matters: this paper's whole thesis is auditable guarantees; conflating "self-audit passed" with externally verifiable properties is the single easiest reviewer-kill in the privacy domain. Minimum fix: one table split into "verified here (method)" and "asserted (unverified)", plus one paragraph on vault-compromise threat and salt management.
- **[MINOR] "$26$ structured PII tags" vs Table 1 "$56$-tag PII span recall benchmark": actual inconsistency confirmed.** Location: abstract, "across all $26$ structured PII tags"; Table 1, ai4privacy row "$56$-tag PII span recall benchmark"; Figure 2 caption, "all $26$ structured tags"; Section 5, "all $26$ structured privacy tags (which include free-form addresses, employers, and usernames)." Three locations say 26, one says 56. The `ai4privacy/pii-masking-200k` dataset card publishes a larger tag universe than 26, so the paper evidently evaluates a 26-tag subset (structured tags only, presumably excluding e.g. organization/medication free-text tags) without ever explaining which tags or why. The "$8$ targeted patterns" inside the $26$ are also unreconciled with the tag list. Minimum fix: one sentence in Section 3 specifying the tag subset and why (free-form/non-structured excluded), and make Table 1 match the abstract.
- **[MINOR] "GDPR Art.~17 blast-radius eviction" is a marketing phrase for KV-cache eviction.** Location: Sections 1 and 3. GDPR Art. 17 erasure is a legal process over controllers and processors; sub-ms KV-slot eviction is a cache-clearing primitive. The over-claim invites a domain reviewer to discount the genuinely sound conformal work. Minimum fix: reword as "cache-slot eviction compatible with Art. 17 erasure workflows" and drop "blast radius" or define it once.
- **[MINOR] PubMedQA eval split provenance**: the 250/250 lexicon split and the 124-injected-contact-token test (Section 5.3, "$124$ within-domain PubMedQA test items with injected contact tokens") are both under-specified: how the 124 items were constructed and where they live relative to the corpus table.

## Seat 4 Perspective (cross-disciplinary, stakeholders, deployment costs)

- **[MAJOR] Stakeholders of failure are never named.** Location: Figure 1 caption, "human review (\texttt{ABSTAIN})," and Section 5.5, "forces the operator to supply additional labeled calibration rows." Two stakeholder groups bear real costs that the paper does not analyze: (i) the human reviewer inside `ABSTAIN`, whose workload at 40.8% abstention (Table 3, `\alpha=0.01`) is not characterized anywhere (what fraction of a corpus, what triage UI, what latency); (ii) the data subject, for whom the paper never discusses what a false `KEEP\_SAFE` release means (see Seat 2's circularity finding: the gate certifies scanner-agreement, not person-level safety). Why it matters: the deployment pitch is auditable conformal release; the party that absorbs both the abstention cost and the false-release risk is unexamined. Minimum fix: one paragraph in Section 7 characterizing abstention workload at the operating points, plus a person-level (not scanner-level) statement of what a wrong certifiable `KEEP\_SAFE` implies.
- **[MINOR] "Zero-egress" boundary is honest in some places and absolute in others.** Location: abstract, "zero-egress document curation" and Table 1 row "Live remote fetch requires outbound network". The abstract sells zero-egress as a property of the system while Table 1 admits live web capture requires outbound connections. A one-word fix in the abstract ("zero-egress default", "egress-gated") avoids being caught by a reviewer reading Table 1.
- **[MINOR] Cross-paper reader experience.** Four companion papers from one codebase is a known risk for readers who encounter one in isolation: Section 5.3's terms (`fair-shot`, `T0+Graph-PPR`, `T2-Chat`) are defined only in the companion paper; `100/100 tests (94 core/Track C tests` (Appendix A) leaks internal experiment names (`Track C`) into a public manuscript. Define each at first use or cite the companion for definitions.
- **A real positive worth keeping:** Table 3's "small calibration sets yield conservative abstention" framing (Table 1 row) and Section 5.5's explicit fail-closed multi-axis story give operators an honest deployment picture, which is rarer than it should be in this genre.

## Seat 5 Devil's Advocate

- **[CRITICAL] The core claim has a hole exactly where the paper's own evidence stops.** The paper's implicit chain: deterministic regex redacts -> conformal gate certifies -> exports are safe. The paper's own Section 4 shows the first arrow captures only 31% of structural PII across tags, and Section 3's labels (Seat 2's circularity) are scanner-derived, so the gate cannot catch what the scanner missed. The canary experiments (Section 5.4, email/SSN/phone/name on an audit note) happen to target exactly the formats regex catches, so the end-to-end safety path is never tested in the miss regime. If the paper's core claim is "deterministic + conformal = safe local curation", then as stated it is not established; what is established is "deterministic + conformal = guaranteed behavior relative to a weak scanner, demonstrated on scanner-catchable threat classes". That is a real but much smaller claim, and the paper should either run the miss-regime experiment or restate the claim once, plainly, where made (which is also what style ruling (c) requires).
- **[MAJOR] Small-n evidence carrying universal phrasing.** Abstract-level claims built from: 8 PMC PDFs ("Deterministic ingestion extracts readable prose from all $8/8$"), 12 OAIC probes ("identical compliance QA accuracy ($75.0\%$, $9/12$ each)"), 3 adversarial prompts x 12 spans, 50 questions. The "zero QA accuracy loss" summary is a binomial draw at n=12; an exact 95% CI on 9/12 spans roughly 38%-93%, so "identical" is within noise, not measured equivalence. Why it matters: MLSys reviewers routinely kill equivalence claims without equivalence tests (TOST) or CIs. Minimum fix: scope to "no accuracy difference observed over 12 probes", or run a TOST/approximate equivalence test, and report CI on the canary deltas.
- **[MAJOR] Deduplication and ingestion micro-benchmarks are toy inputs.** Location: Section 2.1, "strips raw HTML boilerplate by $53.7\%$ ($503$\,B raw HTML to $233$\,B clean prose) ... extracts $150$ characters from a $295$\,B native \texttt{.docx}$". A 295-byte docx and a 503-byte HTML fragment are fixtures, not documents; a 5-input dedup run measures essentially nothing statistical. Why it matters: "millisecond startup latency" and dedup claims become marketing rather than measurement when the corpus is hand-crafted singletons; Table 1's "not TB streaming" caveat is admission enough. Minimum fix: report one ingest-throughput run over the 1,207-doc corpus (MB/s, total wall time) instead of, or alongside, the micro-fixtures.
- **[MAJOR] "Argues with its own past drafts" (violates binding ruling (d)).** Location: Section 5.3, "removes the $92.0\%$ immediate-EOS collapse observed in an earlier flat $49.4$-token baseline". "Earlier" baselines from previous draft rounds are internal history; presenting them inside a submitted paper is precisely the frame ruling (d) prohibits, and `T1`/`T2-Chat`/`fair-shot` jargon is undefined in this manuscript. Minimum fix: delete the collapse history here (it is the companion's contribution), and define or drop the T-arm labels.
- **[MINOR] Cherry-picking check, run deliberately:** the paper does report unfavorable results prominently (31% recall, -0.062 F1 tax, 1.05% leakage, fail-closed abstention, 0%-kept conjunction). This paper is not cherry-picked; its problem is scope, not selection. Credit where due.
- **[MINOR] "So what" test:** the strongest version of the surviving claim (regex recall ceiling + fail-closed LTT gate with correct finite-sample math + fast WASM substrates) is genuinely useful to a narrow operator audience, and is fine for MLSys if Sections 2-4 alone carry the paper once Section 5.3 is compressed to a citation.
- **[MINOR] "$1{,}050$ anonymized responses" denominator is unexplained.** Location: Section 5.4. 70 questions x 2 arms x 3 models = 420, so 1,050 implies additional conditions (likely grid adapters from the companion) never described here. State the decomposition.

## Editorial Decision

**Major Revision.**

Rationale: the unique core (Sections 2, 3, 4: deterministic ingestion, 2D tiling geometry, regex recall ceiling, fail-closed LTT gatekeeping, substrate table) is technically correct as far as recomputable, unusually honest about its own failures, and appropriate for MLSys. But the paper as submitted has (i) a CRITICAL duplication problem with the companion paper in Section 5.3, (ii) a CRITICAL label-circularity problem that undercuts what "certified safe" means, (iii) a CRITICAL reproducibility failure (no artifacts, seeds, or document identifiers), and (iv) no external baseline for its central ceiling claim. All four are fixable, which is why this is not Reject; none is cosmetic, which is why this is not Minor Revision.

## Consensus and disagreements

- All five seats agree on the Track C duplication (Seat 1 CRITICAL), the missing external NER baseline (Seat 3 MAJOR), the label circularity (Seat 2 CRITICAL, Seat 5 CRITICAL, Seat 4 MAJOR), and the reproducibility gap (Seat 2 CRITICAL, Seat 3 MAJOR). These are the decision-driving findings.
- Seat 1 vs Seat 2 mild disagreement on whether the six/five model-count issue is a standalone MAJOR or a symptom that resolves itself when Section 5.3 is compressed under the companion citation; recorded as MAJOR so it is not lost in the cut.
- Seat 5 rates the small-n equivalence claims as MAJOR; Seat 2 rates them MINOR given the companion's CIs partially exist. Weighted toward MAJOR because the abstract makes the claim.
- The panel deliberately did not manufacture balance: the paper does have real weaknesses (Sections 2-4's scale), but it is also genuinely clean on arithmetic and self-disclosure, and the report reflects both without inflation.

## DA CRITICAL adjudication

Two CRITICAL findings from Seat 5's seat (the claim-chain hole and circularity, R2) were cross-examined. The panel confirms them. Counter-consideration given: the paper does explicitly frame regex as "a fast first-pass redactor" and does put a neural tagger in its Limitations, so awareness exists. It does not change the adjudication: the abstract and Figure 1 sell `KEEP\_SAFE` as certified export, and no location scopes the certificate to the labeling rule. The adjudicated claim the paper can currently make is "guaranteed finite-sample control of the format-validated labeling risk," and the fix is either R2's recalibration or a one-time scoping sentence at each claim site plus the miss-regime test. No manufactured balance: the paper is not cherry picked (Seat 5 MINOR finding stands), and the fix is proportionate.
